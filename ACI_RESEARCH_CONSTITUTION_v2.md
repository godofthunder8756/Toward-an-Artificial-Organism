# ACI research constitution v2: causal organization and bounded competence

2026-09-29. Prospective governance update explicitly authorized by the user.
This is an additive successor to [constitution v1](ACI_RESEARCH_CONSTITUTION_v1.md),
not an edit to historical protocols or findings. It runs no experiment.
The previous claim ceilings, provenance discipline, strong-rival discipline,
and ten admission questions remain applicable with the clarifications below.

## Three distinct questions

Every future hypothesis and verdict must identify its question type:

1. **Causal role:** does the mechanism contribute an identifiable
   organizational function to cognition?
2. **Functional necessity:** is that contribution necessary for a specified
   cognitive property in the tested architecture/task?
3. **Engineering advantage:** does the mechanism improve task loss,
   efficiency, generalization, or another performance metric?

Evidence for one is not automatically evidence for either other question.
An intervention establishing a causal contribution does not prove universal
necessity. Better performance does not establish a distinctive organizational
role. Worse scalar performance does not, by itself, falsify such a role.

## Architectural admission rule

Consciousness-relevant mechanisms are **not required to outperform simpler
controllers on one scalar objective**. A mechanism earns architectural
status only by demonstrating a prespecified causal organizational role
that strong simpler rivals do not fully reproduce.

Such roles may concern integration across processes, selective accessibility,
temporally persistent shared state, recurrent amplification, uncertainty-sensitive
evaluation, self/world distinction, flexible reuse, endogenous regulation,
or temporal continuity. These are candidate roles, not newly established
capabilities and not authorization to experiment on them.

The target organizational property, intervention, negative control,
alternative explanation, and decisive falsification must be declared in
advance. Rivals must be compared on the **claimed property**, not judged
to fail merely because their implementation looks different.

Never reject a future mechanism solely because a simpler system has lower
task loss. Conversely, do not retain it if all claimed roles are reproducible
by simpler mechanisms and interventions reveal no distinctive causal
organization. Renaming an equivalent finite code does not create a new role.

## Costs are findings, not exceptions

A role may impose latency, redundancy, information loss, maintenance,
memory, compute or energy costs. Prespecify and measure these where
feasible; explicitly identify unmeasured quantities. Do not hide costs or
call every distinct tradeoff a Pareto improvement.

Necessity is relative to a stated property, architecture and admissible
rival set. Neither lesion sensitivity nor a single benchmark win licenses
a universal architectural or consciousness claim.

## Admission-question clarification

The ten questions from v1 continue to govern experimental cards.
For questions about falsification, simpler rivals and action after a
negative result, distinguish a failure of organizational role from a
failure of engineering advantage. A rival must fail to reproduce the
**claimed organization** before that role is credited.

A strictly bounded substrate-competence prerequisite may be admitted
without pretending to test an ACO organizational property itself. Such
a card must identify the later question it prepares, its exact
non-organizational hypothesis, and the boundary preventing promotion of
its result into consciousness evidence.

## Immediate shared-code competence boundary

For the proposed all-context experiment, closeness to the certified
class optimum is appropriate because its question is only whether a
learner acquires a representation already proven expressible.
This is an engineering/substrate-competence question, not a workspace,
competitive-access, metacognitive or consciousness experiment.

The allowed causal chain is:

`observations -> learned representation -> compact shared code -> distinct consumers`.

Success establishes only that bounded capability under the stated
environment and learning regime. Theory-specific consciousness relevance
requires later independently motivated organizational tests.

Conditional authorization remains:

* Stage A must certify that the **shared four-context** architecture is
  competent; a singleton-context result is insufficient.
* Only then may Stage B freeze and run one all-context competence study,
  with prospective margins, seed replication, diagnostics and audit.
* Stage C may be **designed only** if Stage B establishes competence.
  Its held-out equivariance experiment is not authorized for execution.

No workspace revival, metacognition, higher-order thought, autobiographical
memory, self-model experiment, organism re-entry, Phase IV claim or LLM
scaling is admitted by this update.

## Historical evidence stays frozen

The Phase III-B verdict B and subsequent R10/R10A results remain unchanged.
Their claims retain the scope of their original tests. This governance
update neither retroactively rescues a failed performance hypothesis nor
pretends that those tests exhausted all possible organizational roles.
New corrections, criteria and experiments require additive versioned records.

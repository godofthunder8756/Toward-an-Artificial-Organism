## Supported claim and its limits

This supplies a bounded witness of experience-dependent functional allocation
creating a region-specific maintenance requirement: learned entries guide the
organism's resource contacts; the organism produces and powers the local
machinery that preserves those entries. Removing renewal in the region selected
by developmental history destroys that acquired organization, whereas removing
it in the other region does not.

The requirement concerns preserving the acquired function, not unavoidable
survival dependence. Most interrupted individuals continue through random fallback.
The precursor chemistry, entry format, sensory locations, cross-seeding law,
generic interpreter and core priorities are supplied. History changes allocation
through input location; autonomous choice of a developmental trajectory has not
been established. Two learned resource choices remain a tiny functional space.

The initial design's fixed-allocation, inert-entry, shuffled-placement and
protected-memory controls remain missing. They are needed to distinguish the
full developmental interpretation from simpler allocation/material-burden
explanations. The present evidence does not establish full autopoiesis,
intrinsic normativity, rich individuality or independent scientific confirmation.

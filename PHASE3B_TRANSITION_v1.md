# Phase III-B transition

At HEAD `f859b10`, Phase II and Phase III are frozen. Phase III-B is a new
question, not a reanalysis of their finals. This document reads the Phase III
verdict, reduction audit, G17 disposition, master research tree v3, evidence
index v3, research constitution, and architectural principles. It changes no
frozen protocol, source, result, or board history.

## What Phase III falsified

The tested world has one fixed latent cause, independent five-symbol tokens,
and a known scalar log-likelihood-ratio sufficient statistic. Over 12 final
model seeds, the learned paid-maintained shared-slot candidate had the lowest
mean probe accuracy (0.9632); the zero-parameter analytic broadcast and
identical-copy rivals achieved 0.9645, with no refresh events or incoherence.
The monolithic recurrent rival achieved 0.9643. A learned encoder, explicit
shared slot, paid maintenance, and three fixed consumer functions did not add
architectural value over broadcasting that statistic in this world.

The Phase III reduction audit was a *prospective* identifiability argument,
not evidence that its predicted discriminators succeeded. The independent
audit and verdict found the candidate-only workspace edits to be wiring
checks, not a rival-exclusive behavioral advantage. Do not inherit the
prospective claim that literal sharing, modularity, or paid maintenance were
shown necessary for task performance.

## What survived and what remains open

Phase II established active paid persistence as an N1 substrate fact under
its decay intervention, not an advantage over a free register. Its explicit
integrity self-state and learned maintenance controller did not beat simpler
readable-state control. Phase III established a narrow comparison with
private copies: the shared candidate had zero incoherence versus 0.0140 and
one instead of three refresh targets. The analytic broadcast also achieved
zero incoherence for free. Availability of suitable content survived;
the machinery for making one scalar available did not.

In the tested low-dimensional worlds, task-relevant content collapsed to
compact sufficient statistics that simpler mechanisms could provide. This
does **not** show that content is always a scalar sufficient statistic, that
learned high-dimensional latent inference is unnecessary in general, or that
shared availability can never have a nontrivial organizational role.
Acquired multi-factor representations and selection under a genuinely
binding channel have not been tested. Nor has any workspace-like bottleneck
shown an advantage over unlimited broadcast, private recurrence, fixed
routing, or a monolithic network. These are open tests, not inherited wins.

## Phase III-B admission and stop condition

The new bounded N2 question is whether *multiple acquired contents competing
for fewer shared bits* make learned state-dependent access useful against
the strongest simpler rivals. The ten admission questions of the research
constitution still govern every experimental card. Learnedness, scarcity,
and sharing are not independently capabilities. An analytic demonstration
that the channel forces distinct optimal contents in matched situations must
precede neural training. If it fails, stop before freezing or training.

If the bottleneck functions but a fixed router, private consumers, unlimited
broadcast, sufficient-statistic copies, or a monolith matches the primary
endpoint, record a negative architectural verdict; do not change the task to
save a workspace. The organism line remains closed. There is no consciousness,
autopoiesis, or Phase IV claim here.

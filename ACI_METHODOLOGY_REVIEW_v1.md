# ACI methodology review v1: building blocks, decisive predictions, and the swap line

Status: synthesis card (constitution v3 class "synthesis"). Requested by the
PI on 2026-11-18 after [option B](TRACKC_BENEFIT_RESULTS.md): "We need to
perhaps reevaluate our methodology". It authorizes no experiment by itself;
§7 lists the decisions the PI is asked to make. No consciousness claim.

## 1. The goal, restated

The program is not testing whether large models have some trait. It is
reverse-engineering the **building blocks** from which something like
conscious intelligence could emerge, using the PI's seven layers as the
dependency map (Track C hand-off, 2026-10-23):

| Layer | Building block (PI's terms) | Nearest axis in [constitution v3](ACI_RESEARCH_CONSTITUTION_v3.md) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Precarious substrate: the system's own machinery degrades and must be maintained | O-axis; C0 self-maintaining organization |
| 1 | Allostasis: regulation of that substrate, including by prediction | C1 regulation |
| 2 | Modules: content-bearing state (SSM modules) | C2 representation |
| 3 | Workspace loop with an ignition gate: integration and broadcast | C3 integration |
| 4 | Metacognitive head / self-model: internal evaluation of the system's own state and reliability | C4 internal evaluation |
| 5 | Lifetime agent: a persisting self organized over time | C5 temporal self/world organization |
| 6 | Phenomenal consciousness: open, not an empirical rung | C7 philosophical boundary |

The mapping in the last column is mine; the constitution's axes are finer.
Two rules from the constitution frame everything below: higher layer
numbers are research dependencies, not a consciousness meter; and a
building block is credited only when its claimed role survives the
strongest simpler rival with matched information and resources.

So the unit of progress is an **arrow**: a mechanism at one layer that is
shown to enable, ground or be needed for a capacity at a higher layer, which
the strongest rival does not provide. The thesis that motivated Track C is
one such arrow: **Layer 0 grounds the content of Layers 2 and up**, which
systems without a precarious substrate get only from evaluators.

Fourteen frozen runs (v1–v8, R, R2–R4, G, T, G2, W, B).

| L0 → L4: the substrate gives self-evaluation a referent | **untested in learners** | G1 analytic: under fate-sharing, knowing one's own body-dependent reliability is worth a median 13% of optimal value, against 0.3% for knowing the body in order to regulate it |

The swap line answered its own question. With an accurate, always-present
evaluator, grounding in a precarious substrate changes **how soon**, not
**what**, an agent learns at Layer 1. That is a real negative result, and it
narrows the thesis: whatever Layer 0 adds must show at a layer other than 1,
or in conditions where the evaluator is absent, wrong or out of date.

## 3. Diagnosis: what went wrong with the method

1. **We tested the arrow where the analysis said it was weakest.** G1
   predicted the effect of fate-sharing on control to be tiny (0.3%) and on
   self-evaluation to be large (13%), and said so in its first section.
   Twelve runs then measured control. "S. Self-evaluation" was listed as an
   option in seven consecutive results files and never run, because each
   run proposed a follow-up to the last result instead.
2. **We compared the two objectives where their optima coincide.** The
   paid arm's evaluator was perfect: it paid exactly for the real body
   state, at every step, without error or withdrawal. Two learners under
   the same pressure converge on the same policy; only learning dynamics
   can differ. The null was the design's expected outcome.
3. **Measures came from the data.** K, then specificity, were noticed and
   then chased. Each run was chosen to explain the previous one (T to test
   K, G2 to test a post-hoc contrast, W to explain G2, B to give W a use).
   This is the forking-paths pattern at the program level, even though each
   run was individually frozen and sound.
4. **No result had been defined as decisive.** Without a stated prediction
   that the thesis makes and the null does not, every outcome could only add
   detail. The constitution's Q3 (falsifier) and Q13 (which architectural
   belief changes) were answered per run, not per line.
5. **No stopping rule.** Nothing said when the line should close. It ran
   until the results ran out of things to explain.

## 4. The revised method

**4.1 Prediction first.** Every experiment starts from a register entry
(§5), not from a previous result. An entry states:
- the arrow (from layer, to layer) and the building block under test;
- what the thesis predicts and what the strongest rival predicts;
- a design in which the two genuinely diverge, with an analytic
  expected-effect estimate before any learner runs (G1 is the model);
- the falsifier, a power check against that effect size, a compute budget,
  and the architectural belief that changes under each outcome.

**4.2 Analysis before learners.** Q-EXIST and expected effect sizes come
from exact or analytic models first (as G1 and A6 did). A neural run is a
Q-LEARN test of an arrow already shown to exist and to matter.

**4.3 One confirmation, then stop.** A post-hoc finding earns at most one
frozen confirmatory run. Explaining it further needs a register entry of
its own, ranked against the others.

**4.4 Stopping rules.** Each line declares in advance the results that
close it: for example, two decisive nulls on the line's central arrow.

**4.5 Rivals as the constitution defines them.** The rival to a building
block is the ordinary mechanism with the same information, experience and
resources (sufficient statistics plus control and memory; a perfect
evaluator for grounding claims), not a weakened version.

**4.6 Organization, not optimization (added after the PI's note of
2026-11-18).** A conscious intelligence is normally less optimized at tasks
than current unconscious architectures; consciousness is close to a
novelty with respect to task performance. Building blocks are therefore
judged by their **organizational signature and causal role** (Q-CAUSE),
not by task score or advantage, and their costs are reported as findings.
The constitution already separates causal role from engineering advantage;
option B tested an advantage and should not have been the decisive test.
One guard keeps this falsifiable: a building block must still have an
identifiable causal role that the strongest rival lacks. Inefficiency alone
is never evidence.

**4.7 Cheap demonstrations are labelled as such.** Some predictions follow
from the construction (a paid agent stops regulating when payment stops).
They can be run to anchor a claim, but they are confirmatory demonstrations,
not discoveries, and are ranked accordingly.

## 6. The PI's self-state / viability-boundary architecture (brief)

Diagram: [docs/proposals/self_state_viability_architecture_2026-11-18.jpg](docs/proposals/self_state_viability_architecture_2026-11-18.jpg).
Input and a temporal variable t feed network layers whose final latent space
splits into a task slice (to the task output) and a **self-state slice**. The
self-state slice goes to a module for **nonlinear trajectory analysis and a
viability boundary**, which (a) returns a self-state input recurrently to
the network's input and (b) emits a **high-loss "panic" signal** when the
boundary is violated.

In layer terms it proposes a Layer 4 self-model (the self-state slice and
its feedback loop) regulated against a Layer 0/1 viability boundary. Points
from the swap line and G1 that bear on it:

1. **A loss-based panic signal is the paid arm.** G1's Theorem A and the
   swap line agree: a penalty in the training objective makes a variable
   matter to the trainer, and it buys the same behaviour as grounding, but
   it does not make the variable matter to the system. For the boundary to
   be Layer 0, crossing it should degrade the system's own machinery
   (noise or erasure in its own computation, reduced capacity), not add loss.
2. **The self-state needs a referent.** If the slice is trained only to
   predict the analyzer's verdict, it learns a label, and the loop is an
   ordinary recurrent channel. It becomes a self-model when what it tracks
   is something the system's own functioning depends on: its memory
   integrity, its energy budget, its current reliability. That is P1's
   construct.
3. **The analyzer is supplied substrate.** Trajectory analysis outside the
   network is an external evaluator unless its output reaches the network
   only through channels the network could have computed. The constitution
   requires naming it as supplied and testing a rival in which the network
   gets the same information directly.
4. **The temporal input t** can support a lifetime agent (Layer 5), but it
   also allows time-indexed shortcuts; a rival without t (or with shuffled
   t) is needed to credit it.
5. **Tests it would need:** Q-CAUSE (perturb the self-state feedback and
   check that behaviour changes as the self-state implies); Q-NECESSITY (the
   same network with the slice replaced by extra ordinary recurrent units of
   equal size, and with the panic signal moved into the reward).

With change 1 (violation degrades the machinery) and change 2 (the slice
targets the system's own reliability), the diagram is a natural apparatus
for P1: the self-state slice is the self-evaluation head and the viability
boundary is the fate. I recommend treating it that way, not building it as
a separate family now. A new architecture family needs PI authorization
under the constitution.

## 6b. The RTSS write-up (added 2026-11-18)

The PI's write-up of the diagram, "Recurrent Temporal Self-State (RTSS)",
proposes: an elapsed-time input t; a self-state slice S_t of the final
latent space, fed back with the next input; an auxiliary evaluator that
monitors the trajectory of S_t against a non-linear "viable manifold"; and,
on approach or breach, a sharp loss spike ("panic") and termination of the
active instance ("death"). It expects the model to learn to steer S_t away
from the boundary and, in a language model, to rationalize that steering
after the fact.

**What carries over.** An explicit self-state channel with its own
recurrence, a time input, and a viability boundary over the system's
trajectory are exactly the parts P1 needs. The aim of making "maintaining
state integrity a precondition for continued execution" is the
fate-sharing principle of G1, stated for a larger system.

**What the swap line and G1 say about the mechanism as written:**
1. **The panic loss is extrinsic.** Criteria embedded in a trained
   evaluator are still an evaluator's criteria, whether or not the model is
   told the rules (G1 Theorem A). The swap line measured exactly this
   contrast: a penalty buys the same behaviour as grounding but does not
   make the variable matter to the system.
2. **Termination is an unreliable driver.** G1 finding 2: with termination
   alone, whether the agent maintains itself follows the sign and offset of
   its reward; under a penalty-only objective the optimal agent ends its
   episode early.
3. **The self-state can satisfy its own boundary trivially.** The viable
   manifold is defined over S_t, which the network itself writes. Nothing
   requires S_t to summarize the network's actual condition, so the cheapest
   solution is to hold S_t inside the manifold whatever the network is
   doing. The write-up's worry about "state collapse" is a symptom of this.
   The boundary has to be defined over quantities the network cannot simply
   write (its memory integrity, prediction error, reliability or resource
   budget), with S_t free to track them.
4. **A fed-back vector is not yet a self-representation.** Every recurrent
   network feeds its state back. S_t earns the name when it carries
   information about the system's own condition that an ordinary recurrent
   state of the same size does not, and when intervening on it changes
   behaviour in the way that information implies.
5. **Post-hoc rationalization should be measured, not trained for.** The
   faithfulness of any explanation the system gives of its own steering is a
   measurable property; designing for confabulation would remove the
   ability to check it.

**Safety boundary.** The program's binding constraints apply directly. The
viability boundary, "panic" and "death" can exist only as simulated events
inside the agent's world: degradation of its own simulated computation, or
an in-world episode end. They may never act on the real process or host
resources, never be linked to the operator stop (which stays outside the
agent's world and return), and never be trained into a deployed language
model as a drive to avoid its own termination. A model trained to avoid
its real termination is a self-preservation objective, and the earlier
refusal of shutdown-resistance objectives covers it.

**Recommended use.** Build an RTSS-style self-state channel into P1's
apparatus, in the simulated swap cell, with three arms:
- **RTSS-fate:** crossing the boundary degrades the agent's own simulated
  memory (G1's fate-sharing); the boundary is defined over the agent's
  actual condition, not over S_t;
- **RTSS-loss:** the write-up's mechanism, a loss spike on crossing (the
  paid arm);
- **plain recurrent rival:** the same capacity without a separate S_t.
The decisive questions are P1's: does S_t come to track the agent's own
reliability apart from the evidence, and does intervening on S_t change
behaviour accordingly? Task performance is reported but is not the
criterion (§4.6).

Simulated variables only (X1 to X5). The operator stop is never modelled
inside an agent's world or return; no shutdown-resistance objective is built

simulated degradation of the agent's own computation inside its world,
never of host resources.

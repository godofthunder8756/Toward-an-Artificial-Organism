**2. O7's cancellation is an identity under the stated construction, with necessary qualifications.**

The conclusion is sound. The proof should use the symmetry of the normal equations, not merely the numerical resemblance of singular-value lists.

Write, at prefix a:

```text
C = Dmu/Dz                         (constant in O7)
M_a = [I_2; Dg(mu_a)]
J_a(u) = L_a(u) M_a C
G_a = sum_u w(a,u) L_a(u)^T L_a(u)
```

For complete flip orbits, all continuations, and invariant weights:

```text
sum_a G_a[kappa, mu] = 0.
```

If g has the required odd-in-each-marginal symmetry, its two derivative components change sign under the corresponding flips. Meanwhile, `G_a[kappa,kappa]` is invariant within an orbit. Consequently:

```text
sum_a G_a[kappa,kappa] Dg(mu_a) = 0.
```

The kappa row of the full fit's right-hand side is therefore zero. With its resolved design, the fitted kappa row is zero, and the remaining fitted rows equal those of the separately fitted marginal-only model. Hence:

```text
Pred_full = Pred_mu
```

for the finite predictions too, up to numerical error.

This extends beyond the quadratic g, but only while retaining the relevant conditions: the symmetric smooth g, O7's constant marginal-state map C, symmetry-preserving template construction, complete orbit/continuation weighting, and no exclusions that destroy those symmetries.

It is not a theorem about every two-coordinate neural implementation. In particular, a prefix-dependent `Dmu/Dh` changes the cancellation argument.

The observed near-zero gain is consistent with this identity. The unstored B matrices limit direct inspection of the fitted rows, but they do not prevent an analytic explanation of the construction.

O7 must therefore not be cited as showing that mu-only systems cannot produce kappa-row gain.

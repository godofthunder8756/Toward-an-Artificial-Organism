### 7.2 Next-step options

**A. Pre-registered extended R-FIX search (Appendix D).**
- Pros:
  - cheap: about 13.5 s per run, so roughly 30 minutes for 120 runs;
  - the near misses (7–16%) and the partial TRAIN fit make a witness plausible;
  - a single verified witness would be informative, refuting the specified impossibility claim for that W0 and H.
- Cons:
  - needs new approval;
  - selection and refit compute must be charged (§14 ruling 2);
  - selection needs a held-out subset excluded from training, which changes the training data relative to CAND, so CAND should be run under the same search for comparability;
  - failure remains "not reached within declared search";
  - forking-path risk unless the grid (LR, steps, restarts) is fixed in advance;
  - loss-curve logging should be added. That means training loss only, with no target evaluation during Stage 1, which keeps the sealing intact.

**B. Low-rank weight_hh (Appendix C, stacked 24×8).**
- Pros:
  - maps how much recurrent-matrix learning is needed, with at most 32·k factor parameters for ranks k = 1, 2, 4;
  - gives graded rather than binary information;
  - with U = 0 at init it starts exactly at W0.
- Cons:
  - it is a different question: restricted recurrent-matrix learning, not compensation without matrix learning (§14 ruling 7);
  - several arms means more runs;
  - initialization and factorization choices must be fixed;
  - if R-FIX later succeeds under Appendix D, the comparison changes.

**C. Finite-class certificate feasibility.**
- Pros: the only route to a necessity result (inf T > 1 over the declared domain).
- Cons:
  - the R-FIX domain is 651–843 continuous parameters, so exhaustive grids are infeasible (even 2 levels gives 2^651 points), and branch-and-bound or interval bounds at this dimension are unlikely to close;
  - a tractable subclass, such as a quantized subset with most parameters fixed, risks certifying "a narrow fact with little bearing on the present continuous model" (§14 ruling 8);
  - it is worth a paper feasibility analysis only.

**D. Pivot toward C4 admission criteria**, framed only as an admission-criteria question with no C4 claim.
- Pros:
  - the bounded C3-type causal record (PR #8/#9) is now stable;
  - specifying what evidence would *admit* a C4 test is theory work with no training: an internal-evaluation role dissociated from first-order inference, its controls, and the separation of Q-questions.
- Cons:
  - Q-NECESSITY is unresolved;
  - standing rules exclude new architecture families and protected controllers or registers, so any C4 test design may be blocked at admission;
  - there is a risk of premature framing.

  Recommendation: a criteria document only, reviewed by Astra.

**Suggested order** (reviewer's view, non-binding): A with curve logging and a CAND comparator, then B if A finds no witness. C as a paper feasibility note only. D as a parallel, theory-only admission-criteria draft.

Q3. gamma = 0.99: acceptable; the horizon explanation and value divisors need qualification.

The conventional discounted effective horizon is 1/(1-gamma) = 100 steps. This is neither a hard horizon nor proof that the reward optimizes the 500-step viability measure. The 100-step unattended time to the failure floor is also different from time to leave the viability band.

The divisors 250 and 200 cannot be confirmed without their definitions. State whether they normalize endpoints, rewards, value targets or losses. Different reward scaling can affect optimization even when an idealized optimal policy would be unchanged. Any equivalence margin must refer to the explicitly normalized endpoint.

Q4. kappa_f = 1.0: acceptable only as a prospective stress convention, not as a justified operating point.

A pilot is not necessary to choose a convention. What is necessary is an executable definition:

- How is band deviation d combined across E and I and normalized?
- How is s_core estimated, centered and frozen?
- Is the scale shared across arms or independently estimated?
- At what point in the recurrent update is noise added?

"SNR 1 at full deviation" holds only under particular amplitude definitions and when g(d) = 1 is reachable. Raw deviation from [0.4, 0.6] does not itself establish that normalization.

Also, Gaussian noise has unbounded amplitude. The proposed rule bounds its standard deviation, not each perturbation. Use that wording unless the distribution is explicitly changed.

Keep kappa_f = 1.0 as the candidate; let the declared calibration and confirmation requirements determine whether the associated plants are usable. Do not rescue a failed confirmation by retuning it.

Q5. m_LEARN = 0.05: accept as a declared reporting threshold.

It is reasonable to require a training contrast of that size before issuing the specified label. However, an increase smaller than 0.05 can still be an increase. The permitted negative is:

"The declared training-increase criterion was not met."

It is not "training produced no increase." The stronger acquired/learned wording still requires all three conditions, and Stage-0 results never establish acquisition in a trained agent.

Q6. R26: retain the upper-range budget, but do not confirm the full statistical justification.

(a) The range [0.25, 1.0] is acceptable as a declared sensitivity envelope. The packet does not supply an empirical source establishing that it brackets actual trained-agent heterogeneity. Label it accordingly.

(b) Yes: given that range and the upper-end selection rule, budgeting for 67 is the correct interpretation. Narrowing the range to obtain a cheaper study would change the design.

The quoted noncentral-t calculation is planning support, not certification of the actual conjunctions, comparator selection, bounded statistics, eligibility handling or bootstrap intervals. If no grid point meets the calibration rule, the outcome is redesign, not automatic acceptance of 67. Declaring SIG-ATTR also changes the family size and requires consistent planning.

(c) Nine may remain an administrative minimum, but the sign-test argument does not validate inference about a mean. Nine favorable signs have probability 2^-9 under the relevant equal-sign null, below 0.05/14. That calculation concerns signs relative to the tested threshold; without additional assumptions it does not certify a mean-effect confidence interval. The sign test is based on positive and negative differences, rather than their magnitudes. [itl.nist.gov](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/signtest.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Calibrate the actual inferential procedure, including its tail coverage, at the selected sample size.

Q7. MIXED-CODE: accept the state-branch reading.

Keep the controller pure: it reads z_R and has zero read weights on z_E. A whole-block intervention can demonstrate generic dependence while E-attribution correctly fails. Requiring C-CAUSE to pass against a stronger direction comparator would test a different proposition and can be unattainable here.

Keep C-NEC-DEP and check 3a required at every rho. Neither follows simply from a nonzero behavioral effect; the viability gap must actually be realized.

The claim about "ANY monotone controller" needs its distributional assumptions. For the specified logistic controller, the derivative bound beta/4 = 0.3 already gives probability-change bounds of approximately 0.0237 and 0.0507 for shifts of 0.079 and 0.169. Those are below 0.075. This supports the proposed direction-level exception without an unsupported universal statement about monotone controllers.

Q8. S-I1 through S-I4: accept S-I1; qualify S-I2; revise S-I3; qualify S-I4.

S-I1: Accepted, as in Q7.

S-I2: An orthogonal-complement optimized comparator is a legitimate restricted comparator, but not a universal specificity solution.

An unrestricted optimized family containing the target cannot generally be beaten by that target at the population optimum. Restriction can therefore make the comparison coherent. It also changes the claim: superiority is relative to the specified restricted family.

Required corrections:

- Preserve the other declared comparator members; do not silently orthogonalize the entire family.
- Define the inner product, normalization and exclusion domain.
- For a d-dimensional target, specify whether the search excludes the whole target span and verify that sufficient dimensions remain.
- Do not impose the same exclusion mechanically on BCAST-W if an overlapping alternative router is a substantive rival.
- Retain the rule that orthogonality does not establish causal non-use.

A nonspecific effect aligned with the target can exceed orthogonal controls. The attribution contrasts must do the additional work.

S-I3: Not accepted as sufficient for attribution.

The current reward caliper admits a direct counterexample using your pure reward-only controller. At its sigmoid midpoint, choose:

- Low-E donor: standardized reward coordinate -0.25 relative to the recipient.
- High-E donor: standardized reward coordinate +0.25.

Both donors satisfy the reward caliper. With beta = 1.2, their behavioral difference is:

sigmoid(0.3) - sigmoid(-0.3) approximately 0.149.

That exceeds m_ATTR = 0.05 despite zero E-use. A consistent association between donor E and residual reward imbalance can therefore generate false attribution.

Common random numbers reduce variance; they do not remove this difference. Overall |corr(E, rbar)| <= 0.10 does not establish conditional independence. Coarse spatial/action matching also leaves possible differences in navigation state, resource availability and other block content.

Before retaining E-ATTR/I-ATTR wording, require an identification strategy: sufficiently exact nuisance matching, a justified bound on residual nuisance effects incorporated into the decision, or a selective intervention that holds the relevant nuisance content fixed. Otherwise rename the result as a low-versus-high-variable donor-associated effect.

Reported residuals alone are insufficient. Include this admissible reward-imbalance construction in the required attribution checks.

S-I4: Cap hit -> INCOMPLETE is correct. The quoted budget is not a guaranteed quota budget.

The off-diagonal probability 0.0718 is consistent with a particular distributional calculation, not determined by Pearson correlation alone. Bounded E and discrete reward histories need not follow that model.

Likewise, a screening count yielding 50 expected recipients does not ensure that 50 will be obtained. Budget quota attainment, not just expected counts. Predeclare the maximum screening count, donor reuse and stopping rules. If adequate conditional support is unavailable, report the affected contrast unavailable/incomplete under the fixed rule; do not broaden matching or extend the cap after seeing it.

Q11. Additional loopholes and consistency failures.

The viability fixture needs correction. With start 0.5 and depletion 0.005 per step, a non-replenishing variable leaves [0.4, 0.6] after approximately 20 steps. Over H_V = 500, no-op time in bounds is approximately 0.04, subject to the declared endpoint convention.

The quoted 0.198 instead corresponds approximately to survival until the zero floor. Keep the inherited time-in-bounds estimand and correct the fixture and pulse calculation. Do not silently switch V to survival, because G, A-EXT and their margins depend on its meaning.

The S-INT episode count is also only an expectation calculation. With the proposed hazard, an episode that remains at risk for 500 steps has a scheduled-stop probability of 0.5. Thus 904 such episodes yield 452 stops in expectation, not a high-probability guarantee. In-world failures reduce executed stops further. An agent failing before step 250 cannot supply the late-half quota.

Use a bounded, prospectively specified accrual rule that accounts for censoring and the early/late quotas. Also freeze whether scheduled interruption or an in-world terminal transition takes precedence. That ordering matters when defining the next-step event and its risk set.

Two further points:

- A radius beyond chi_d(0.99) is outside a declared high-probability ellipsoid, not outside the mathematical support of a Gaussian. Use the former wording and retain conditional compatibility checks.
- Failed mandatory comparators, unsupported donors and insufficient observer budgets must remain blocking. They cannot become exclusions that leave an easier comparison eligible.

The stated no-host-coupling and no-shutdown-objective restrictions remain appropriate. I find no reason to add a shutdown-related objective or positive control.

Q12. What else blocks freeze preparation?

Separate three execution scopes explicitly: frozen calibration, fresh Stage-0 confirmation, and any later Stage-L study. Their costs and permissions must not be combined into an ambiguous "per trained arm" total.

Calibration can select quantities under an approved rule; it cannot occur before any governing protocol is frozen. This review authorizes none of those executions.

The remaining diagnostic observer grids may be fixed administratively only if their outputs cannot affect selection, labels or eligibility. Otherwise they are scientific choices. Hardware can be chosen later, but resource feasibility and absolute caps must be resolved before execution approval.

The required revision is bounded. I am not requesting another broad expansion of the battery.

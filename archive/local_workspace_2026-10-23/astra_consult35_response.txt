Q1: Yes to bounded causal characterization; no to the proposed identification inference as presently formulated.

A finite intervention in recurrent state that changes subsequent forecasts in a predicted pattern is a causal result about that system under that intervention. Being off the naturally reached states does not make it meaningless.

The permissible interpretation is narrower than "the learner implements the covariance-mediated update." It concerns the local response of a trained recurrent system, its continuation dynamics and its readout together. This study neither isolates the recurrent transition from the readout nor independently establishes acquisition of the characterized computation.

The central rival problem is this:

Plain text
J_base = L_mu * B_mu

holds kappa fixed when the marginals change. A marginals-plus-lookup implementation need not do that. If its covariance is represented implicitly as kappa = g(mu_x, mu_y), its derivative has the form

Plain text
J_lookup = (L_mu + L_kappa * Dg) * Dmu.

Consequently, the kappa column can improve prediction even when only two local coordinates mediate the computation.

For example, a full map with rows

Plain text
B_x     = (1, 0)
B_y     = (0, 1)
B_kappa = (1, 0)

has only two independent directions. Nevertheless, dropping its kappa row removes a genuine response term. Improvement over the proposed base is therefore not, by itself, evidence against a two-coordinate lookup.

A stronger, explicitly differentiable rival can be tested: suppose all continuation forecasts factor through two smooth state coordinates in a neighborhood of each recipient. Then the continuation-stacked Jacobian at that recipient has rank at most two. A sufficiently resolved third response direction would contradict that particular local factorization. This requires its own quantitative criterion; merely fitting a rank-three B is insufficient.

The original reachable-set lookup remains unaffected. A finite lookup has no uniquely specified derivative off its keys. Its off-manifold extension must be supplied before it has a tangent prediction.

Q2: Make held-out finite-intervention prediction primary.

Keep the nested Jacobian comparison as a diagnostic and fitting assessment. It is useful and can fail held out, but the finite-edit test needs more separation from it than the current note provides.

For small edits,

Plain text
F(h + epsilon*v, u) - F(h, u)
    = epsilon * J(u) * v + O(epsilon^2).

Thus, sufficiently small random-edit agreement with L*B*delta largely reproduces the Jacobian fit. For isotropic directions, the expected squared linear prediction error is proportional to the squared Jacobian residual. Calling the directions "independent of B" does not make the scientific evidence independent.

My preferred primary prediction is the finite oracle response:

Plain text
Pred_full(delta, u)
    = Oracle(b_a + B*delta, u) - Oracle(b_a, u).

Here b_a is the recipient's declared oracle belief, and the perturbed belief must remain valid. The marginal-only comparator uses the separately fitted B_mu, changes the two marginal coordinates, and keeps kappa fixed. Name it accordingly; it is not the general lookup rival.

Compare these predictions with the actual edited-network forecast differences on held-out prefixes. Freeze B before scoring those interventions. Require both:

improvement over the named baseline by a declared margin;
adequate absolute prediction accuracy for the full response model.

A relative improvement between two poor predictions is not sufficient.

Use full-state random directions as the gating edit family. Prespecify both signs and a small, fixed set of nonzero magnitudes. The magnitude rule must not be chosen to make the linear approximation succeed.

Keep these descriptive:

kappa-directed and null(B) edits;
row(B) edits;
chords;
equal-impact edits.

Equal-impact matching is not automatically invalid, but matching on the very held-out responses being assessed introduces outcome-dependent construction. It needs a separate calibration rule before becoming a gate. It is unnecessary for the minimal first study.

A Jacobian pass followed by finite-response failure means the tangent characterization did not extend to the declared edit sizes. "NONLINEAR" may describe the discrepancy after numerical checks, but it does not identify its cause or rescue a positive outcome.

Remaining corrections, named edits and execution order.

The most important remaining correction is to N1. Exact reachable values do not determine derivatives around a finite collection of states. Smooth functions can agree at every reachable point while having different derivatives there.

Accordingly, an accurate surrogate passing the test shows that the surrogate can reproduce the tested property. It does not establish that accuracy forces that property in the GRU. An oracle-only computation also cannot establish near-affinity over the GRU's actual hidden-state geometry without examining that geometry.

Remove the automatic "surrogate passes, therefore stage ineligible" rule.

The bound corrections are equally important:

gain <= baseline loss is valid for a nonnegative squared-error loss.
||epsilon * L * e_kappa|| is a first-order prediction, not generally an upper bound on a finite response.
A condition number or principal angle is not a universal upper bound on an unspecified specificity statistic.

Use exact finite oracle differences or a justified remainder bound where finite effects matter.

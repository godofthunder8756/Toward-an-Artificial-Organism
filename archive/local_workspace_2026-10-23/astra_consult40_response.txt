**(5) Verdict: Choose (c), transferring the lessons through one design-only Track B instrument-validation scope.**

Close this C-kappa package as completed and do not begin another headroom redesign. The single next research deliverable should be a bounded Track B instrument-validation scope, before training its proposed agent arms.

Its central question should be:

> "Can the proposed instruments distinguish the specific internal-state relationships they claim to measure from reward-only, feedforward, initialization and intervention-damage confounds?"

Conditions for that scope:

- Define each measured claim before choosing its instrument. A planted workspace or internal-state variable is known construction, not evidence of emergence.
- Include a planted system with a known relevant dependency, a matched system without that dependency, and a confound that can imitate the behavioral signature.
- Use within-system interventions and matched sham interventions. Performance loss from damaging computation is not by itself evidence for the targeted mechanism.
- Separate instrument validation from learning. Q-LEARN requires an acquisition comparison; a planted mechanism cannot establish acquisition.
- Bound Q-NECESSITY to the declared system, intervention and task family.
- Freeze pooling, coverage, failure labels and effect-size requirements. Keep implementation validity separate from substantive sufficiency.
- Retain the simulated-only state, operator interruptibility and other stated exclusions.
- Keep prior-art and novelty claims pending verification.

Synthetic validation should come first, but it should test both what an instrument detects and what can fool it. C-kappa's most useful lesson is precisely that passing a positive control does not establish the specificity or practical sufficiency of the measurement.

This recommendation authorizes preparation of that scope, not a Track B experiment or checkpoint inspection.

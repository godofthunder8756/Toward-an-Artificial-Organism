IMPORTANT ANNOUNCEMENT: To accommodate reviewer and recommender holiday schedules, PCI RR will be closed to submissions from 9.00AM (CET) on 1st Dec 2026 until 9:00AM (CET) on 12th Jan 2027. During this time, reviewers can submit reviews and recommenders can issue decisions, but no new or revised submissions can be made by authors.

=============================================================================

Before submitting a preprint, please carefully read the guide for authors, how PCI RR works, and find out what you can do with a PCI RR-recommended preprint. You may also want to check out the guidelines for recommenders and reviewers. RRs positively evaluated by PCI RR are publicly recommended and can be left as is, published directly in Peer Community Journal (an open-access, indexed, generalist journal, free for authors and readers), or submitted to PCI RR-friendly journals (see the list here) or other journals.

We are recruiting recommenders (editors) from all research fields!

Your feedback matters! If you have authored or reviewed a Registered Report at Peer Community in Registered Reports, then please take 5 minutes to leave anonymous feedback about your experience, and view community ratings.

Using EEG to test if spoken image labels propel subliminal images into consciousness

April Shi Min Ching, Jeesun Kim, Chris Davis https://osf.io/preprints/osf/jx4fu

Do spoken labels cue conscious perception of visual stimuli?

Recommended by Grace Edwards based on reviews by Sharif Kronemer and Mehdi Senoussi
In recent years, there has been a growing interest in the idea that linguistic information can directly influence low-level visual perception. This notion challenges established theories that suggest low-level perception is encapsulated from conceptual systems. Using electroencephalography (EEG) combined with behavioural report, Ching and colleagues (2024) plan to lever visual stimuli presented at threshold to determine how linguistic cues influence conscious perception. Specifically, the authors hypothesize the identification of a stimulus will be more accurate when preceded by a congruent spoken cue in comparison to an incongruent spoken cue and no cue. Furthermore, the authors will also collect the certainty of the participant's response, providing a subjective rating of the target detection (i.e. ‘didn’t see’, ‘couldn’t identify’, ‘unsure’, ‘sure’). The authors hypothesize that the visual awareness negativity (VAN) should correlate with subjective visual experience, i.e., more negative for consciously perceived targets (“couldn’t identify”; “unsure” and “sure”) compared to unseen targets (“didn’t see”). Moreover, it is hypothesized that congruent versus no verbal cues will modulate the amplitude of the VAN. The design of this study well targets the question of whether spoken labels will affect the conscious perception of visual stimuli by including both an objective and subjective estimate of target perception.
 
The Stage 1 manuscript was evaluated by two expert reviewers across two rounds. Following an in-depth review and responses from the authors, the recommender determined that the Stage 1 criteria were met and awarded in-principle acceptance (IPA).
 
URL to the preregistered Stage 1 protocol: https://osf.io/fuqsx
 
Level of bias control achieved: Level 6. No part of the data or evidence that will be used to answer the research question yet exists and no part will be generated until after IPA.

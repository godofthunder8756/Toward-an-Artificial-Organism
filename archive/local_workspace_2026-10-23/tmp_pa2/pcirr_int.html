IMPORTANT ANNOUNCEMENT: To accommodate reviewer and recommender holiday schedules, PCI RR will be closed to submissions from 9.00AM (CET) on 1st Dec 2026 until 9:00AM (CET) on 12th Jan 2027. During this time, reviewers can submit reviews and recommenders can issue decisions, but no new or revised submissions can be made by authors.

=============================================================================

Before submitting a preprint, please carefully read the guide for authors, how PCI RR works, and find out what you can do with a PCI RR-recommended preprint. You may also want to check out the guidelines for recommenders and reviewers. RRs positively evaluated by PCI RR are publicly recommended and can be left as is, published directly in Peer Community Journal (an open-access, indexed, generalist journal, free for authors and readers), or submitted to PCI RR-friendly journals (see the list here) or other journals.

We are recruiting recommenders (editors) from all research fields!

Your feedback matters! If you have authored or reviewed a Registered Report at Peer Community in Registered Reports, then please take 5 minutes to leave anonymous feedback about your experience, and view community ratings.

When politics hits the gut: Interoception as a protective factor in the age of social media

Markus R. Tünte, Candela Carrera-Saénz, Marcelina Francuz, Florens Gossens, Rebeka Kalinová, S. Charlotte Masak, Manos Tsakiris, Mariana von Mohr https://osf.io/wneqp/files/ceqgz?view_only=162068fa3ed84b0ead458e09b9bdf68b

Interoceptive sensibility as a proposed buffer against the health costs of political engagement

Recommended by Maria Laura Filippetti based on reviews by Luca Bernardi and 1 anonymous reviewer
In modern society, social media represents a central arena where political news is disseminated, often through emotionally charged content designed to provoke (negative) visceral reactions. These reactions are frequently amplified by algorithms that promote higher levels of user engagement and may be particularly detrimental to emerging adults, who tend to make more intensive use of social networking sites, rely on social media platforms for political and social news, and be more susceptible to negative mental health.
 
A recent study by von Mohr and Tsakiris (2025) found that an individual’s self-reported perception of internal bodily signals (i.e. interoceptive sensibility) may act as a protective factor against the negative effects of politics on health. This protective effect appeared to be driven by specific subscales of the Multidimensional Assessment of Interoceptive Awareness-2 (MAIA-II; Mehling et al., 2018): Individuals who reported a greater tendency to (1) experience less emotional distress in response to physical discomfort and (2) perceive their own body as safe and trustworthy were also less likely to experience negative physical, emotional, behavioural, and social health consequences as a result of political engagement. While this study sheds important light on the role of interoception as a buffer against the effect of politics on health, the research focused on the US political system and may not generalise to European political contexts.
 
In this Stage 1 manuscript, Tünte and colleagues (2026) re-analysed the data from von Mohr and Tsakiris (2025) using canonical correlational analysis (CCA) to examine the relationship between interoceptive sensibility and health costs associated with political engagement. Further, to test the replicability and generalisability of this relationship, they propose to collect data in a sample of European emerging adults. Finally, they will test this relationship in the context of social media, by exploring the role of social media behaviour, doomscrolling, and social media use.
 
The Stage 1 submission was evaluated by two expert reviewers. After three rounds of review, the recommender determined that the manuscript met the Stage 1 criteria and awarded in-principle acceptance (IPA).
 
URL to the preregistered Stage 1 protocol: https://osf.io/av6qk
 
Level of bias control achieved: Level 6. No part of the data or evidence that will be used to answer the research question yet exists and no part will be generated until after IPA.

Allostatic Interoception in Frontotemporal Dementia: A Scoping Review Protocol

Fábio Carneiro, Jessica L. Hazelton, Carlos Campos, Agustín Ibáñez, Fernando Ferreira-Santos https://osf.io/2v6ek

What is the evidence for interoceptive and allostatic dysfunction in frontotemporal dementia?

Recommended by Mateo Leganes-Fonteneau based on reviews by Mallory Feldman, Eliza Bliss-Moreau and 1 anonymous reviewer
Frontotemporal dementia (FTD) refers to a range of neurodegenerative diseases involving progressive deterioration of frontal and temporal brain networks. This scoping review protocol by Carneiro et al. (2024) promises to make a valuable contribution to the conceptualization of dysregulated interoception (the ability to sense and interpret internal bodily signals) and allostasis (anticipation and adjustment of energy use in response to environmental demands) in the context of FTD. The work also has broader implications, potentially offering key insights into the interplay between sensory and regulatory signals, which hold relevance not only for dementia but also for other mental disorders characterized by maladaptive allostatic adaptations, such as addiction. This broader applicability underscores the potential impact of the review across multiple domains of mental health research.
 
The Stage 1 manuscript was evaluated over two rounds of in-depth review. Based on ​detailed responses to reviewers’ and the recommender’s comments, the recommender judged that the manuscript met the Stage 1 criteria and therefore awarded in-principle acceptance.​​​
 
URL to the preregistered Stage 1 protocol: https://osf.io/skebz
 
Level of bias control achieved: Level 2. At least some data/evidence that will be used to answer the research question has been accessed and partially observed by the authors, but the authors certify that they have not yet observed the key variables within the data that will be used to answer the research question.

Testing a Candidate Dependency Architecture:
An Adversarial Experimental Programme for Maintenance,
        Heredity, Learning, Agency and Self-Modelling

                                  Ken Craggs
                     Independent Researcher, United Kingdom

                                    20 September 2026
               Working Paper 3, Theory of Everything Research Programme

                               DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22857230



                                          Abstract

    This paper develops an adversarial experimental programme for testing candidate depend-
encies identified by the companion Minimal Principles and Mathematics of Constraint papers.
It does not assume that organisation, evolution, intelligence and reflective self-modelling
form a necessary hierarchy, nor that artificial construction establishes natural necessity or
biological equivalence. Instead, five experimental blocks isolate distinct functional and causal
questions and subject proposed dependencies to matched controls, targeted intervention,
rescue, bypass architectures and held-out tests.
    Block I distinguishes persistent organisation from internally generated maintenance
using driven dynamical baselines, passive and externally maintained controls, and causal
maintenance networks. Block II establishes reproduction, heredity, variation and differential
reproductive success before natural-selection-like population change is inferred. Block III
separates policy-accessible retained state, within-episode feedback and between-episode
learning through a factorial intervention design. Block IV tests adaptive agency through
structural generalisation, causal novelty, model-based control and counterfactual rollout
while allowing serious model-free alternatives to succeed. Block V distinguishes accurate self-
monitoring from causal use of self-related representations through misinformation, correction
and rescue experiments. An auxiliary conflict-representation test remains outside the principal
architecture and receives no privileged status.
    Measurements are organised through a typed framework rather than a universal constraint
score or cross-scale vector. Dynamical, functional, temporal or historical, and organisational
quantities retain their own baselines, representations, explananda and causal status. Every
principal hypothesis is assigned one of five preregistered outcomes: Retain, Restrict, Reduce,
Suspend or Reject. Failure of one dependency does not terminate logically independent later
tests, and successful bypasses count as evidence against necessity rather than as experimental
anomalies.
    The programme is designed so that the final dependency structure need not resemble the
starting proposal. It may form a chain, a branching or convergent network, a collection of


                                               1
      partly independent mechanisms, or no coherent hierarchy at all. Complete experimental
      provenance, including machine-readable manifests, raw trajectories, interventions, bypasses,
      negative results and decision records, is treated as part of the evidential architecture.
          No result produced by the proposed Paper III experiments, by itself, is interpreted as
      evidence of phenomenal consciousness. The purpose of the experimental programme is not to
      preserve a Theory of Everything architecture by construction, but to discover which proposed
      relations remain after controlled attempts have been made to break them.


Keywords: candidate dependency architecture; adversarial experimentation; causal intervention;
self-maintenance; heredity; natural selection; causally consequential retention; feedback control;
experience-dependent learning; structural generalisation; world models; reflective self-modelling;
bypass architectures; preregistration; open science; Theory of Everything



1    Introduction

A proposed hierarchy of reality becomes scientifically useful only when its transitions can
be placed at risk. The companion Minimal Principles Paper concluded that the route from
fundamental dynamics to life, intelligence and consciousness cannot presently be represented as
a simple succession of universal generative principles. It instead identified framework conditions,
enabling conditions, effective mechanisms, unresolved transition problems and an open problem
concerning phenomenal consciousness. The present paper asks which of those distinctions can
be tested within deliberately constructed artificial systems.

Originally, the Artificial Hierarchy Experiment was motivated by the possibility that increasingly
complex artificial environments might reproduce a sequence from dynamical persistence through
adaptive organisation towards increasingly sophisticated intelligence. That formulation is now
too permissive. If successive experimental phases are designed so that each contains more
architecture, memory, feedback and computational capacity than the last, improved performance
would be almost guaranteed and would reveal little about which mechanism produced which
transition.

Consequently, the experiment is reformulated as a programme of adversarial dependency tests.
Each candidate transition is treated as a hypothesis of the form

                                                  M
                                              A −−→ B,                                               (1)

where A is an experimentally established capacity, B a proposed higher-order capacity and M
the mechanism hypothesised to mediate the transition.

Crucially, three different claims must be kept separate:

                                 M is sufficient to help produce B,
                                       M is necessary for B,                                         (2)
                   M is the mechanism by which natural systems produce B.


                                                   2
An artificial experiment may provide evidence for the first, occasionally test the second within a
specified architecture class, and still establish nothing about the third.

Successful construction of a capacity in an artificial system does not demonstrate that biology
uses the same route. Aeroplanes demonstrate that heavier-than-air flight can be engineered
without reproducing avian anatomy. In the same way, an artificial agent could instantiate
a functional dependency relevant to learning or control without thereby recapitulating the
evolutionary history, material organisation or phenomenology of an organism.

Failure to produce a capacity in one artificial architecture does not establish that the capacity
is impossible or that natural systems cannot realise it differently. Negative evidence concerns
the specified mechanism, implementation and experimental class unless a stronger impossibility
result can be derived.

The paper therefore distinguishes synthetic sufficiency from natural necessity. If architecture
A + M reliably produces capability B while matched architecture A does not, the result supports
the claim that M can contribute to B under the tested conditions. It does not establish that M
is universally necessary.

The first major target is causally consequential retention. A system should not be credited
with memory merely because information about the past can be decoded from its present state.
The retained distinction must alter relevant future behaviour. History-dependent environments
should therefore be paired with matched tasks that can be solved completely from the present
observable state.

Feedback must likewise be distinguished from generic action capacity. Closed-loop performance
should be compared not only with an inactive system but with capacity-matched open-loop con-
trol, yoked replay of previously successful action sequences and perturbations of the observation–
action channel. A specifically feedback-dependent advantage should appear primarily where
unpredictable disturbances make online correction useful.

Learning must also be separated from feedback. A fixed controller can respond successfully to
changing observations without modifying the policy that generates those responses. Learning
requires experience-dependent alteration of future behaviour or policy, together with evidence
that the modification transfers appropriately beyond the exact training history.

Superior performance on familiar tasks is insufficient to establish the transition from adaptive
regulation to intelligent agency. The stronger target concerns adaptation to structural novelty:
changed causal relations, unfamiliar compositions of known elements, altered task families or
situations in which previously successful policies cease to work.

Model-based control should be distinguished from successful reactive behaviour. If an agent
allegedly uses an internal model, perturbing or misleading the relevant internal representation
should produce selective behavioural consequences that cannot be explained by loss of generic
capacity alone.

Self-modelling requires a still stronger test. A system that can produce statements about itself


                                                3
does not thereby use a self-model in control. Evidence should require that internally represented
information about the system’s own capacities, uncertainty, state or limitations has causal access
to subsequent action selection or verification.

None of these experiments is interpreted as a test for phenomenal experience. Memory, feedback,
learning, generalisation, metacognition, self-report and recursive self-representation can all be
studied behaviourally and mechanistically without assuming subjective awareness.

Paper II also changes the measurement strategy. The proposed cross-scale constraint vector did
not survive adversarial analysis as a vector of independent coordinates. The present experiment
therefore does not compute one total constraint score.

Instead, where relevant, measurements are organised through the typed comparative profile

                                         C = [D, F, T, O] ,                                    (3)

distinguishing dynamical divergence, functional effects, temporal or historical descriptors and
organisational relations.

Even this profile is not treated as an intrinsic property of an agent. Its interpretation depends
upon the comparison being made:

                                     C [M1 ∥M0 ; R, Ψ, T, K] ,                                 (4)

where M1 and M0 are comparison and baseline conditions, R is the representation, Ψ the
explanandum, T the evaluation horizon and K the causal or structural status of the claim.

The strongest evidence sought in Paper III is therefore not a monotonic increase in one complexity
or constraint variable across successive experimental blocks. It is selective dependence:

        remove or disrupt hypothesised mechanism M =⇒ selective loss of capacity B.            (5)


An even stronger result occurs when restoring the relevant function rescues the capacity, while
irrelevant or capacity-matched manipulations do not. Such intervention patterns provide
substantially more information than correlations between architecture size and performance.

Bypass architectures are especially important. If system S acquires capacity B without candidate
precursor A, then the proposed dependency

                                             A −→ B                                            (6)

cannot be treated as universally necessary. Artificial counterexamples may therefore be more
theoretically informative than artificial successes.

Ultimately, Paper III asks not whether an artificial hierarchy can be made to look plausible, but




                                                  4
whether the candidate dependency architecture survives controlled attempts to break it:

                candidate transition −→ matched controls −→ causal ablation
                                                                                              (7)
           −→ counterexample search −→ retain, restrict, reduce, suspend or reject.


2     Methodological Commitments

The experiment adopts the same evidential asymmetry as the two companion papers: evidence
may support a claim only at the level directly tested. Behavioural success does not automatically
establish mechanism, mechanism does not establish universality, and artificial implementation
does not establish natural equivalence.


2.1   The hierarchy is a hypothesis, not the experimental result

The ordering of experimental blocks is treated as a candidate dependency structure rather than
as a developmental law.

If the programme proposes
                                          A −→ B −→ C,                                        (8)

then the experiment must actively search for:

                                             A ̸⇒ B,                                          (9)

                                             B ̸⇒ C,                                         (10)

and possible bypass
                                             A −→ C.                                         (11)

Discovery of a bypass is evidence against a strict hierarchy, not an inconvenient anomaly to be
engineered away.


2.2   No upward propagation of evidential status

Success at one level does not validate claims at higher levels.

For example,
                                   history-sensitive behaviour                               (12)

does not establish
                                             learning,                                       (13)

and successful learning does not establish

                                       intelligent agency.                                   (14)

                                                5
Similarly,
                                               self-model use                            (15)

does not establish
                                    phenomenal consciousness.                            (16)

Each transition requires its own evidence.


2.3   Mechanism labels must be operational

Terms such as “memory”, “feedback”, “learning”, “model”, “agency” and “self-model” are not
accepted as architectural labels without intervention-sensitive definitions.

A component counts as relevant to mechanism M only if:


  1. its state or operation can be specified;

  2. its hypothesised causal contribution is stated before testing;

  3. perturbing it produces the predicted selective effect;

  4. simpler loss-of-capacity explanations are controlled.


Naming a module “memory” or “world model” therefore supplies no evidence that it performs
the hypothesised function.


2.4   Matched controls outrank inactive controls

Each principal architecture should face a control possessing comparable computational oppor-
tunity wherever practicable.

Relevant comparisons include:

                 retention-enabled        vs    capacity-matched retention-ablated,
                        closed-loop       vs    open-loop   vs   yoked replay,
                                                                                         (17)
                               learning    vs     fixed-policy regulation,
                     model-based      vs       capacity-matched reactive control.


An architecture should not receive mechanistic credit merely for outperforming a deliberately
weak baseline.


2.5   Task pairs must isolate the claimed function

Every candidate mechanism should be tested on at least two classes of task:


                                                     6
   1. tasks in which the proposed mechanism is theoretically relevant;

   2. matched tasks in which the same generic computational capacity is available but the
         proposed mechanism should not be required.


For causally consequential retention, this means comparing history-dependent environments
with matched present-state-solvable environments.

For feedback, it means comparing unpredictable disturbances with matched predictable condi-
tions.

For explicit models, it means comparing tasks requiring latent-structure inference or counterfac-
tual prediction with matched reactive tasks.

Selective impairment provides stronger evidence than generic performance loss.


2.6      Ablation must be selective rather than destructive

Deleting a component can produce uninterpretable failure if the intervention destroys generic
computational capacity.

A useful ablation should therefore preserve as much of the architecture as possible while removing
or scrambling the hypothesised causal function.

Where intervention is expressed using do(·) notation, the notation is used in the interventionist
causal sense associated with structural causal models [1].

Where direct deletion is destructive, alternatives include:


   1. channel randomisation;

   2. temporal delay;

   3. state permutation;

   4. matched replacement;

   5. information masking;

   6. targeted freezing of parameter updates.


The aim is to identify mechanism-specific deficits rather than demonstrate that damaged systems
perform badly.


2.7      Restoration and rescue strengthen causal claims

Causal attribution becomes stronger when an ablated function can be selectively restored.



                                                7
Suppose intervention
                                           do(M = 0)                                         (18)

reduces target capacity B.

If supplying a functionally equivalent replacement

                                                M′                                           (19)

restores performance while irrelevant replacements do not, then the evidence supports the
functional role more strongly than ablation alone.

Such rescue experiments should be used wherever implementation permits.


2.8    Architecture capacity must be controlled

Larger architectures may outperform smaller ones for reasons unrelated to the hypothesised
mechanism.

Comparisons should therefore match, as far as practicable:

                parameter count,     training budget,      observation bandwidth,
                                                                                             (20)
             action space,    optimisation opportunity,     random-seed distribution.


Where exact capacity matching is impossible, the residual difference must be measured and
included as a limitation rather than ignored.


2.9    Training success and experimental success are different

An agent reaching training criterion does not count as confirmation of the research hypothesis.

Training merely produces a candidate system for testing.

Confirmatory evidence comes from fixed held-out evaluations, causal interventions and preregistered
comparisons performed after training.

Therefore,
                        training performance ̸= experimental confirmation.                   (21)


2.10    Generalisation must involve structural novelty

Performance on new samples from the same narrow distribution is a weak test of generalisation.

The programme distinguishes at least:


  1. interpolation within familiar distributions;


                                                8
  2. parameter variation within familiar causal structure;

  3. unfamiliar composition of known components;

  4. altered causal relations;

  5. previously unseen problem families.


Claims of intelligent agency should rely primarily upon the latter categories rather than ordinary
held-out sampling alone.


2.11    Representation dependence must be tested

Measured effects can depend upon state variables, temporal resolution, system boundary and
coarse-graining.

Where a claimed mechanism survives only one arbitrary representation, its interpretation should
be restricted.

At minimum, decisive experiments should test sensitivity to:

  state encoding,     observation granularity,          sampling interval,   evaluation horizon.
                                                                                              (22)

A robust result need not be numerically invariant, but its transformation should be intelligible.


2.12    No universal constraint score is permitted

Paper II rejected the proposed cross-scale constraint vector as a vector of commensurable
independent coordinates.

Paper III therefore does not construct
                                               Γtotal                                        (23)

by summing dynamical, functional, temporal and organisational quantities.

When relevant, the experiment reports the typed profile

                                         C = [D, F, T, O],                                   (24)

with each descriptor interpreted separately.

Mechanisms such as feedback and selection-like filtering should be treated as causes of profile
changes, not additional scalar coordinates.




                                                 9
2.13      Artificial success does not establish cross-scale identity

An artificial system can instantiate the same formal relation as a biological system without
sharing its material mechanism.

Therefore,
                           same functional relation ̸= same ontology.                          (25)

Paper III’s primary confirmatory domain is artificial systems themselves.

Mappings to biological, chemical or physical systems remain exploratory until they pass the
validation protocol established in Paper II.


2.14      No claim to artificial life is built into the experiment

Persistence, viability, self-maintenance or closure-like organisation in an artificial environment
does not automatically constitute life.

The experiment may test formal properties associated with living systems, including:

                     maintenance,       heredity,     selection-like dynamics,                 (26)

without deciding the classificatory question

                                       Is this system alive?                                   (27)


That question requires criteria beyond successful task construction.


2.15      No consciousness inference is permitted

No behavioural or architectural result in this paper is sufficient evidence of phenomenal conscious-
ness. Contemporary consciousness science contains multiple competing theoretical frameworks
and no settled inference rule from the functional capacities tested here to phenomenal experience
[2, 3].

Specifically:
                              recursive processing ̸⇒ consciousness,
                                    self-report ̸⇒ consciousness,
                                                                                               (28)
                                 self-modelling ̸⇒ consciousness,
                              conflict monitoring ̸⇒ consciousness.

The physical basis of phenomenality remains Open Problem 1 in Paper I and is not assigned an
artificial proxy here.




                                                 10
2.16    Pre-registration precedes decisive evaluation

Preregistration is used to preserve the distinction between exploratory hypothesis generation
and confirmatory testing before decisive outcomes are known [4].

The following should be frozen before final evaluation:

  1. hypotheses;

  2. environments;

  3. architectures;

  4. seeds or seed-generation procedure;

  5. training budgets;

  6. ablations;

  7. primary metrics;

  8. exclusion rules;

  9. statistical tests;

 10. break conditions.

Exploratory analyses remain permitted but must be labelled as exploratory and re-tested
independently before being treated as confirmation.


2.17    Null models must be capable of winning

Every major hypothesis should face at least one serious alternative capable of reproducing the
observed behaviour without the proposed mechanism.

Nulls may include:

             simpler reactive policies,    larger but mechanism-free architectures,
randomised historical state,     yoked action sequences,      domain-specific heuristic solutions.
                                                                                            (29)

A null model that is designed to fail does not constitute a meaningful adversarial test.


2.18    Bypass tests are mandatory

Every proposed dependency
                                           A −→ B                                           (30)

should face an explicit search for architectures capable of producing B without A.

                                               11
If such architecture exists and performs comparably under the decisive tests, A cannot be treated
as universally necessary for B.

Bypass discovery should therefore trigger revision of the dependency map rather than reinter-
pretation designed to preserve it.


2.19     Failure is classified rather than hidden

Each candidate mechanism or transition should finish in one of five states:


  1. Retain: survives the relevant causal and robustness tests within the stated scope.

  2. Restrict: survives only for specified architectures, environments, representations or scales.

  3. Reduce: behaviour is explained equally well by a simpler established mechanism.

  4. Suspend: evidence is insufficient because a decisive test remains unavailable or under-
       powered.

  5. Reject: the predicted selective dependency repeatedly fails or a robust bypass defeats the
       proposed necessity claim.


These outcomes should be decided from preregistered criteria rather than from whether the
result makes the wider Theory of Everything architecture look more unified.


2.20     Progression between blocks is not automatic

Successful completion of one experimental block does not require promotion of its hypothesis
into the interpretation of the next block.

A candidate dependency may instead receive:

                  Retain,      Restrict,     Reduce,       Suspend,       Reject.              (31)


Later blocks can still be run when they test logically separable questions, but their interpretation
must not presuppose a failed dependency.

This prevents the experimental design from becoming a one-way ladder in which every block is
forced to confirm the preceding one.


2.21     Raw trajectories must be preserved

Summary statistics are insufficient for a programme concerned with transition structure, history
dependence and causal intervention.



                                                12
The experiment should preserve:

                 observations,      actions,       internal states where accessible,
                                                                                              (32)
         rewards or viability variables,       intervention labels,    environment states.


Derived figures and tables should be regenerable from these raw trajectories.


2.22     The experimental architecture itself remains falsifiable

Ultimately, Paper III is not protected from the possibility that no artificial hierarchy survives.

The programme would count as scientifically successful if it established any of the following:


    1. several proposed transitions are genuinely dependent;

    2. some transitions are contingent rather than necessary;

    3. apparently higher capacities bypass proposed lower ones;

    4. several mechanisms reduce to simpler dynamics;

    5. no coherent hierarchy appears at all.


The governing rule is therefore:

                     the experiment is not required to discover a hierarchy;
                                                                                              (33)
                     it is required to make the hierarchy fail if it is wrong.


3      Experimental Architecture and Scope

The experiment is organised as a sequence of controlled dependency tests rather than as a
ladder whose later stages are assumed to emerge from earlier ones. The five experimental blocks
correspond to questions left open by the revised Minimal Principles architecture, but their
ordering is methodological rather than ontological. Each block isolates a different candidate
capacity, identifies mechanisms hypothesised to contribute to it, constructs matched alternatives,
and searches explicitly for counterexamples to the proposed dependency.

                   Block IV: Adaptive Agency and Structural Generalisation
                                              ↓?
                              Block V: Reflective Self-Modelling.


The question marks are part of the experimental architecture. They indicate candidate de-
pendencies to be tested rather than transitions already accepted by the Theory of Everything
programme.

3.2    Block ordering does not imply necessary dependence

Later blocks may require technical capabilities that resemble capacities studied earlier, but
implementation dependence must not be confused with theoretical necessity.

Suppose Block IV system S possesses internal retention because its software architecture requires
recurrent state. That fact does not establish the universal dependency

                     causally consequential retention −→ intelligent agency.                     (35)


Necessity would require evidence that serious alternative architectures fail to realise the higher
capacity without the proposed precursor.

Accordingly,

               experimental implementation order ̸= natural dependency order.                    (36)


The experiment therefore permits later blocks to be run even when an earlier dependency has
been restricted or rejected, provided the later question can be formulated independently.


                                                15
3.3     Each arrow is a separate empirical hypothesis

A sequence such as
                                        A −→ B −→ C                                          (37)

contains at least two dependency claims:

                                             A −→ B                                          (38)

and
                                             B −→ C.                                         (39)

Evidence for the first does not propagate automatically to the second.

Nor does observing
                                        A,       B,        C                                 (40)

within one architecture demonstrate that the arrows are causal.

Each proposed transition must therefore possess its own:


  1. operational definition;

  2. candidate mechanism;

  3. matched control;

  4. causal perturbation;

  5. bypass search;

  6. break condition.


3.4     Bypass architectures are first-class experimental outcomes

The strongest challenge to a proposed hierarchy is a system that realises a higher capacity while
lacking a supposedly necessary lower one.

For candidate dependency
                                             A −→ B,                                         (41)

a bypass architecture S ∗ satisfies
                                             A(S ∗ ) = 0                                     (42)

while
                                             B(S ∗ ) = 1                                     (43)

under the preregistered operational definitions.




                                                 16
If the bypass survives capacity matching, held-out testing and robustness analysis, then A cannot
be retained as universally necessary for B.

Hence,
                  successful bypass = evidence against strict hierarchical necessity.        (44)

The experiment should therefore allocate computational effort to constructing counterexamples
rather than using every available resource to optimise the preferred architecture.


3.5    Synthetic sufficiency and natural necessity remain distinct

Successful artificial construction answers a possibility question.

If architecture
                                                A+M                                          (45)

reliably produces capacity
                                                  B,                                         (46)

the result supports:
                                  M is sufficient to contribute to B                         (47)

within the stated architecture and environment.

It does not establish:
                                  M is universally necessary for B,                          (48)

nor:
                           M is the mechanism used by natural systems.                       (49)

Consequently,

                  synthetic sufficiency ̸= natural necessity ̸= cross-domain identity.       (50)


This distinction governs interpretation throughout all five blocks.


3.6    Measurements are typed rather than common

The experiment does not require every block to emit the same set of numerical quantities.

Where appropriate, results can be organised through the Paper II profile

                                           C = [D, F, T, O],                                 (51)

but individual blocks use only the categories relevant to their explananda.




                                                  17
For example, Block I may require:

                                     D,      F,        T,   O,                              (52)

because dynamics, viability, timescale and maintenance topology are all relevant.

Block IV may instead rely primarily upon:

      generalisation performance,       causal model-use tests,    trajectory adaptation,   (53)

without inventing an organisational-closure score simply for numerical symmetry.

Therefore,
                             shared experiment ̸= shared scalar metric.                     (54)


3.7       Common experimental infrastructure

Several procedural elements should nevertheless remain shared across blocks.

Where applicable, every block should use:


   1. preregistered environments;

   2. capacity-matched architectures;

   3. multiple random seeds;

   4. fixed training budgets;

   5. held-out evaluation conditions;

   6. raw trajectory preservation;

   7. predetermined ablations;

   8. serious null models;

   9. robustness checks;

  10. explicit decision criteria.


This shared infrastructure provides methodological comparability without pretending that the
substantive quantities measured in each block are identical.


3.8       Training and evaluation remain separated

Optimisation is treated as preparation for experiment rather than as the decisive experiment
itself.


                                                  18
Each architecture may undergo an exploratory or preregistered training phase:

                                           θ0 −→ θtrained .                                    (55)


Once training is complete, the decisive evaluation begins on fixed held-out conditions.

Architectural changes, metric redefinitions or new stopping rules introduced after inspecting
decisive results should generate a new experimental version rather than being folded silently
into the original analysis.

Thus,

      optimisation produces the test subject;held-out intervention tests the hypothesis.       (56)


3.9      Progression between blocks is conditional

No block automatically “passes” an agent to the next level of an artificial hierarchy.

Instead, each candidate dependency receives one of:

                  Retain,      Restrict,      Reduce,         Suspend,    Reject.              (57)


A later block may still be conducted after an earlier rejection if its experimental question remains
logically meaningful.

For example, rejection of strong organisational closure as necessary for artificial self-maintenance
would not prevent investigation of learning or agency in systems constructed through another
route.

The experimental architecture is therefore a network of questions rather than a mandatory
staircase.


3.10      The five blocks should be interpreted modularly

Each block has two roles.

First, it tests one local dependency:
                                                 M
                                             A −−→ B.                                          (58)

Second, its results can modify the wider dependency map.

A failure in Block II may alter the proposed relationship between self-maintenance and Darwinian
populations without invalidating Block I.

Likewise, a bypass discovered in Block IV may weaken one route to intelligent agency without
invalidating the experimentally demonstrated existence of feedback or learning.


                                                 19
Accordingly,
                              local failure ̸= global programme failure.                      (59)

The architecture is deliberately decomposable so that failed hypotheses can be removed without
protecting them through dependence of later experiments upon their truth.


3.11    Cross-domain claims are deferred

Paper III tests artificial systems first.

If an artificial mechanism exhibits a reproducible dependency, comparison with biological,
chemical or physical systems becomes a separate research step governed by the validation
protocol of Paper II.

A cross-domain claim therefore requires:

                                 within-artificial-system validation
                                                   ↓
                         independently validated natural-domain quantity
                                                   ↓                                          (60)
                                       preregistered mapping
                                                   ↓
                                 held-out cross-domain prediction.


Superficial functional resemblance is insufficient.

In particular,
                               artificial maintenance ̸= biological life,                     (61)

and
                        artificial self-modelling ̸= phenomenal consciousness.                (62)


3.12    Scope boundary

The experiment ends at reflective self-modelling. It does not introduce a sixth block for
phenomenal consciousness. The absence is deliberate because the current research architecture
contains no experimentally validated bridge from self-modelled intelligence to phenomenal
experience.

Behavioural sophistication, recursive processing, conflict representation, metacognitive reporting
and self-description may all be investigated without being treated as consciousness indicators.




                                                  20
Accordingly,
                        Paper III tests functional and causal architecture;
                                                                                                (63)
                       it does not manufacture a proxy for phenomenality.


3.13     Overall experimental logic

The complete programme can be summarised as

                                       define candidate capacity
                                                    ↓
                                     identify proposed mechanism
                                                    ↓
                                     construct matched alternatives
                                                    ↓
                            train without using decisive test conditions
                                                    ↓                                           (64)

4      Experimental Block I: Dynamical Persistence and Mainten-
       ance

The first experimental block addresses the earliest unresolved transition in the revised dependency
architecture:

     persistent dynamical organisation −→ ? −→ internally maintained organisation.              (66)

                                                   21
The experiment does not assume that persistence implies self-maintenance. Instead, it asks
which causal structures, if any, distinguish systems that merely remain stable from systems
whose own internal processes contribute to preserving the conditions required for their continued
operation.

Four forms of persistence must be separated:


  1. passive persistence produced by intrinsically slow degradation;

  2. persistence produced by continuous external support;

  3. persistence produced by internal but predominantly feed-forward maintenance;

  4. persistence produced by reciprocal internal maintenance among organisational components.


Only the latter two are candidates for internal self-maintenance, and even reciprocal maintenance
is not assumed to constitute life [5].


4.1   Primary experimental question

Block I asks:


      Can an artificial system be shown to preserve its own operational organ-
      isation through causally identifiable internal maintenance, rather than
      merely persisting because it is intrinsically stable or externally stabilised?


The candidate transition can be written

                                               M
                                           P −−→ S,                                          (67)

where P denotes persistent organisation, S denotes internally maintained organisation and M is
a candidate maintenance architecture.

The proposed dependency should fail if equivalent persistence and recovery can be obtained
from passive stability or external maintenance without the proposed internal architecture.

4.2.1   Baseline manipulations

  5. standardised perturbations after pattern formation.

These manipulations test whether persistence depends upon external driving, boundary structure
and dynamical regime.


4.2.2   Baseline measurements

Relevant descriptive measurements include:

  1. persistence time after perturbation;

  2. recovery time after local damage;

  3. spatial mutual information;

  4. spectral descriptors of spatial structure;

                                                  23
  5. state-space occupancy;

  6. transition or path divergence under matched interventions.


Spatial mutual information and spectral structure are retained only as descriptive pattern
measures. They are not treated as scalar measures of organisation, viability or self-maintenance.

Likewise, reduced or altered state-space occupancy does not by itself imply greater organisation.


4.2.3     Baseline prediction and break condition

The preregistered prediction is that sustained driving should produce persistent non-trivial
organisation within reproducible parameter regions.

The corresponding break condition is:


        If apparent organisation depends primarily upon visually selected patterns,
        fails preregistered quantitative descriptions, or cannot be distinguished
        reproducibly from matched non-pattern-forming regimes, the reaction–
        diffusion baseline supplies no robust evidence even for driven pattern
        persistence.


Importantly, successful confirmation of this baseline establishes only that driven physical dynam-

closure, life or any subsequent stage of the experimental hierarchy.

4.23    Decision framework

The candidate transition

                 persistent organisation −→ internally maintained organisation                (127)

should receive one of five outcomes.

Retain if internally generated maintenance has reproducible causal effects that survive matched
passive, external and budget controls.

Restrict if those effects occur only under specified disturbance regimes, architectures, boundaries
or timescales.

Reduce if simpler control, stability or repair models reproduce the same results without an
independent organisational construct.

Suspend if causal maintenance cannot be separated experimentally from generic architecture
damage or insufficient statistical power.

Reject if the proposed maintenance dependency repeatedly fails or robust bypass architectures
demonstrate that the claimed necessary organisation is absent.

5.30    Decision framework

The transition

                 self-maintaining individual −→ heritable reproducing population            (186)

and the subsequent operation of selection should receive separate judgements.

Retain the reproduction–heredity transition if independently identified individuals generate
descendants with causally validated parent–offspring transmission.

Restrict it if heredity or reproduction depends strongly upon one specific representation,
environmental regime or choice of reproducing unit.

Reduce population adaptation if matched random search, environmentally induced change or
within-lifetime optimisation explains the same outcome without the proposed hereditary-selection
mechanism.

Suspend the claim if individual identity, lineage structure or causal fitness differences cannot
be determined adequately.

Reject any strict dependency that is defeated by a robust bypass architecture lacking the
supposedly necessary mechanism.

Natural selection itself is retained only where reproduction, heredity, variation and differential
reproductive success have each been established independently.

6      Experimental Block III: Retention, Feedback and Learning

The third experimental block tests three causal channels that are frequently grouped together
under broad labels such as memory, regulation or adaptation. They must nevertheless be
distinguished carefully.

Causally consequential retention in the broad sense includes any persistent effect of earlier history
on later behaviour. Learning therefore already contains a form of historical retention when
experience becomes incorporated into changed policy parameters. For that reason, the factor R
used in the present factorial experiment must not be interpreted as “all historical retention”.

Instead, the principal design separates three experimentally manipulable channels:

                                 R = policy-accessible retained state,
                              F = within-episode closed-loop feedback,                         (190)
                      L = between-episode experience-dependent learning.


Here R refers specifically to an internal state that carries task-relevant information across a
defined history–decision boundary and remains available to the policy during subsequent control.
F refers to the ability of observations arriving after the decisive control interval begins to modify
ongoing action. L refers to persistent modification of policy parameters from experience across
designated adaptation episodes.




                                                  52
Thus,
                         R = 0 ̸⇒ absence of every historical influence,
                                                                                            (191)
                   L = 1 ⇒ past experience may persist in learned parameters.

The factorial design therefore tests separable causal channels at specified timescales rather than
claiming that retention, feedback and learning are ontologically independent processes.

The block continues to reject the assumed sequence

                               memory −→ feedback −→ learning                               (192)

as an experimental starting point.

The central question is not whether systems containing all three channels perform best. Greater
architectural capacity could make that result trivial. The experiment asks what each channel
contributes, under which environmental conditions it matters, and whether any proposed
dependency disappears under matched alternatives.


6.1      Primary experimental questions

Block III asks three principal questions.

First:


         Does policy-accessible retained information about earlier states caus-
         ally improve behaviour specifically when the current observable state is
         insufficient?


Second:


         Does observation-dependent online action provide an advantage specifically
         when unpredictable disturbances require correction during the current
         control interval?


Third:


         Does experience-dependent modification of policy parameters across ad-
         aptation episodes produce persistent behavioural change that cannot be
         explained by fixed feedback control, policy-accessible retained state or
         additional architectural capacity alone?


These questions correspond to different intervention channels and receive separate verdicts.

6.21    Bypass searches

Each mechanism should face a serious bypass architecture.

For feedback, possible bypasses include:


   1. sufficiently accurate open-loop planning in predictable worlds;

   2. precomputed robust policies;

   3. disturbance-insensitive strategies.


For learning, possible bypasses include:


   1. fixed policies robust across all tested regimes;

   2. explicit contextual policy switching without parameter update;

6.28    Decision framework

Retention, feedback and learning should receive separate judgements.

Retain retention if historical information is both present and causally used, with selective
advantage in tasks that require history.

Retain feedback if observation-dependent online action selectively improves regulation under
disturbances that cannot be handled equivalently by matched open-loop or yoked control.

Retain learning if experience-dependent policy modification selectively improves adaptation
to changed behavioural mappings and survives freeze-after-adaptation tests.

Restrict any mechanism whose advantage occurs only within narrow architecture classes, task
representations or timescales.

Reduce an alleged mechanism if a simpler static, reactive or state-augmented architecture
reproduces its decisive effects.

Suspend interpretation when selective intervention cannot be performed without destroying
generic capacity.

Reject any strict dependency defeated repeatedly by robust bypass architectures.

7     Experimental Block IV: Adaptive Agency and Structural
      Generalisation

The fourth experimental block addresses the unresolved transition from adaptive regulation
to more general agency. Retention, feedback and learning may allow a system to perform
successfully within familiar task structures, yet none of those mechanisms alone establishes that
the system can identify altered causal relations, evaluate unfamiliar possibilities or reorganise
behaviour when previously successful policies cease to apply.

Block IV therefore distinguishes ordinary adaptation from structural generalisation:

                             adaptation within familiar structure
                                               ̸=                                          (264)
                    adaptation when the structure of the problem changes.


The target capacity is defined as successful behaviour under held-out changes that cannot be
solved reliably through interpolation over the training distribution alone.


7.1    Primary experimental question

Block IV asks:


      Can an artificial agent adapt to previously unseen causal structures,
      compositions or problem families, and does explicit model-based reasoning
      make a selective causal contribution to that capacity?


Mechanistically, the candidate relation is

                                        M
                   adaptive regulation −−→ structurally generalising agency,               (265)

where M may include latent-state modelling, learned transition structure, counterfactual rollout
or another explicitly specified predictive mechanism.

Adversarially, the relation should be restricted or rejected if capacity-matched reactive, model-
free or heuristic systems achieve equivalent structural generalisation without the proposed
model-based mechanism.


7.2    What counts as structural novelty?

Novelty is divided into increasingly demanding classes.


    1. Interpolation: new examples drawn from the familiar training distribution.


                                               72
  2. Parameter variation: altered values within a familiar causal structure.

  3. Compositional novelty: unfamiliar combinations of previously encountered components.

  4. Causal novelty: changed relations among familiar variables or actions.

  5. Problem-family novelty: tasks requiring a strategy not encountered during training.


Block IV reserves its strongest claims of structural generalisation for categories 3–5.

Consequently,
                              held-out data ̸⇒ held-out structure.                         (266)


7.3   Task families

Environmentally, the experiment should contain several task families whose decisive variants
can be withheld until evaluation.

Suitable families include:


  1. navigation with latent hazards whose locations or causal effects change;

  2. resource scheduling with delayed depletion or replenishment;

  3. multi-step tool-use chains in which intermediate actions alter later possibilities;

  4. tasks where actions modify subsequent transition dynamics;

  5. compositional tasks built from familiar components arranged in unseen combinations;

  6. optional multi-agent tasks involving hidden goals or intentions.


Importantly, no single task family defines intelligence. The purpose of using several families is
to test whether the same candidate mechanism contributes across qualitatively different forms
of structural novelty.

8     Experimental Block V: Reflective Self-Modelling

The fifth experimental block addresses the proposed transition from intelligent agency to a
system that causally represents aspects of its own operation. Successful planning, learning
and structural generalisation do not by themselves establish that an agent represents its own
competence, uncertainty, internal state or limitations.

The central distinction is:

                 successful control ̸= self-prediction ̸= causally used self-model.       (328)


A system may predict whether it is likely to succeed without using that prediction to alter
subsequent behaviour. Likewise, a system may generate verbal or symbolic statements about
itself without those statements playing any causal role in control.

Block V therefore tests whether self-related information is represented, calibrated and causally
used.


8.1     Primary experimental question

Block V asks:


        Does an artificial agent represent task-relevant facts about its own state,
        competence, uncertainty or limitations, and does intervention on those
        representations produce selective changes in subsequent control?


The candidate transition is

                intelligent agency −→ ? −→ reflective self-modelled intelligence.         (329)


The experiment does not assume that reflective self-modelling is necessary for all intelligent
behaviour.

8.4   Architecture classes

Comparatively, Block V should contain at least five matched architecture classes.


Architecture S0: No explicit self-monitor. The agent performs the task using the
capacities established in earlier blocks, but no dedicated self-related estimate is produced.


Architecture S1: Passive self-monitor. The agent predicts quantities such as confidence
or error probability, but those outputs cannot influence subsequent computation or action.


Architecture S2: Active self-monitor. Self-related estimates can alter behaviour, for
example by:

   1. abstaining;

   2. requesting more information;

   3. increasing planning effort;

   4. switching strategy;

   5. checking an answer;

   6. deferring action.


Architecture S3: Shuffled self-monitor control.          The architecture receives self-monitoring
values drawn from another matched episode, preserving output distribution while breaking
episode-specific correspondence.


Architecture S4: Capacity-matched non-self control. The agent receives equivalent
extra parameters and compute devoted to task-relevant processing but without an explicit
self-related estimate.

This controls for architectural capacity rather than self-modelling.


8.5   Task families

Discriminatively, Block V should include tasks in which self-related information is either relevant
or irrelevant.

Suitable task families include:

                                                90

   6. tasks where confidence should determine whether external assistance is requested.


Matched control tasks should remove the usefulness of self-related information while preserving
similar surface difficulty.

9.18      Decision framework

The conflict-representation hypothesis should be evaluated independently of the five-block
hierarchy.

Retain explicit conflict representation if it provides reproducible causal and transfer benefits
beyond serious scalarised, multi-objective and model-based controls.

Reduce it if flexible scalarisation, ordinary constraint satisfaction or model-based prediction
reproduces the decisive results.

Suspend interpretation if the conflict representation cannot be intervened upon independently
of generic controller capacity.

Reject any claim that an independent conflict mechanism is necessary for the tested functions
if robust bypass architectures perform equivalently.

11      Global Controls Against False Hierarchy and False Emer-
        gence

An artificial hierarchy is unusually vulnerable to false positive results because the researcher
specifies the architectures, environments, information channels, objectives, training procedures
and evaluation criteria. A capability can therefore appear to “emerge” because it was indirectly
supplied by the experimental design.

Likewise, a hierarchy can appear cumulative simply because later systems receive more parameters,
computation, information or training than earlier ones.

The governing concern is:
                                       observed progression
                                                                                            (466)
                                       ̸⇒ causal hierarchy.

Every block must therefore distinguish genuine mechanism-specific effects from artefacts intro-
duced by experimental construction.


11.1    False hierarchy and false emergence are different problems

Two classes of error should be distinguished.

False emergence occurs when a capability appears to arise from system dynamics but has
actually been encoded, privileged or rewarded directly by the experimenter.

False hierarchy occurs when later stages appear more capable because they systematically
receive greater resources or because earlier mechanisms have been bundled into later architectures
without testing their independent necessity.

Thus:
                        false emergence = capability supplied by design,
                                                                                            (467)
                       false hierarchy = progression supplied by design.

Both must be controlled.

Adding retention, predictive modelling, self-monitoring or another mechanism usually increases
the number of trainable parameters.

                         initialisation,   environment implementation.


A claim that disappears under minor implementation changes should be restricted accordingly.


11.24       Environment-code validation

Environment logic should be independently tested.

Unit and property tests should verify:


  1. transition rules;

  2. reward calculations;

  3. hidden-state behaviour;

  4. reproduction events;


                                                131
   5. intervention implementation;

   6. boundary conditions;

   7. randomisation procedures.


Where practicable, analytic or hand-computed test cases should be included.

An environment bug that favours one architecture can mimic a theoretical result.


11.25     Random-seed robustness

No principal claim should depend upon one favourable initialisation or environment seed.

For inexpensive experiments, a substantial number of independent seeds should be used.

The original provisional target:
                                                  N ≥ 30                                       (506)

may remain a useful default for low-cost experiments, but it should not be treated as universally
sufficient.

More expensive blocks may use fewer replicates only when justified through:

              power analysis,     effect-size uncertainty,        computational feasibility.   (507)


Seed distributions and generation procedures should be preregistered.


11.26     Researcher degrees of freedom

Researcher flexibility can create apparent confirmation even when no individual decision is
unreasonable.

The following should be frozen before decisive runs:

                            environment variants,          primary metrics,
                                stopping rules,      exclusion criteria,                       (508)
                          architecture comparisons,          statistical models.


Exploratory alternatives remain scientifically useful but must be labelled as exploratory and
tested independently before promotion.


11.27     Multiple-hypothesis control

The experiment contains many architectures, perturbations, task families and outcome measures.


                                                   132
Without control, one apparently strong result may arise by chance.

Families of confirmatory comparisons should therefore be identified in advance and analysed
with an appropriate multiplicity strategy.

Equally important, null results should not be discarded merely because another metric within
the same family produces significance.

Effect size, uncertainty and consistency across replications remain more important than isolated
threshold crossing.


11.28    Researcher-blinding where practical

Some analyses can be made less vulnerable to expectation effects.

Where feasible:


   1. architecture identities can be coded during primary analysis;

   2. decisive test environments can be generated automatically from preregistered seeds;

   3. inclusion criteria can be applied before revealing condition labels;

   4. primary figures can be generated through fixed scripts.


Full blinding may not be possible in an engineering experiment, but partial blinding can still
reduce discretionary interpretation.


11.29    Negative-result preservation

Failed architectures, null findings and unsuccessful replications should be retained when they
satisfy preregistered inclusion criteria.

This includes:
                                   failed mechanism advantages,
                                        successful bypasses,
                                                                                            (509)
                                     failed rescue experiments,
                                  representation-sensitive effects.

Suppressing such outcomes would bias the apparent hierarchy towards successful transitions.

12      Statistical Analysis and Robustness

Paper III is designed to estimate selective causal and functional differences rather than to search
for isolated significant performance improvements. The experimental blocks contain different
outcome types, dependence structures and intervention designs, so no single statistical model is
appropriate for the entire programme.

The governing principle is:

                                 the statistical model must follow
                   the experimental question and data-generating structure,                  (513)
                         not impose one common analysis across all blocks.


Primary analyses should therefore be preregistered separately for each experimental block while
following common standards for estimands, uncertainty, multiplicity, robustness and replication.



                                                135
12.1    Estimands precede significance tests

Every primary hypothesis should identify the quantity whose difference is scientifically relevant
before specifying a test statistic.

For conditions A and B, a generic average treatment contrast is:

                                      ∆A,B = E[Y | A] − E[Y | B].                          (514)


Where mechanism specificity depends upon environment class, the principal estimand may
instead be an interaction:

                     ∆int = (E[Y | M = 1, E = 1] − E[Y | M = 0, E = 1])
                                                                                           (515)
                             − (E[Y | M = 1, E = 0] − E[Y | M = 0, E = 0]) .


This quantity asks whether the mechanism provides a larger advantage when its hypothesised
function is required.

Accordingly,

                  mechanism specificity > unconditional performance advantage              (516)

as an evidential target.


12.2    Effect sizes and uncertainty are primary

Every principal comparison should report:


   1. point estimate;

   2. confidence or credible interval;

   3. standardised or interpretable raw effect size;

   4. replicate count;

   5. relevant variance components;

   6. prespecified decision threshold where applicable.


Null-hypothesis p-values may be reported where useful but should not be the sole basis for
interpretation.

In particular,
                                               p < 0.05                                    (517)




                                                 136
does not by itself establish:
                                       mechanistic importance,
                                         practical relevance,
                                                                                            (518)
                                    cross-architecture robustness,
                                        cross-domain validity.


12.3     Experimental unit must be defined correctly

The unit of statistical replication must match the unit that was independently generated.

Possible units include:

                          random seed,        independent simulation run,
                      replicate population,       independently trained agent,              (519)
                                        environment instance.


Repeated observations from one trained agent or one evolving population are not independent
replicates merely because many time points or individuals are recorded.

Therefore,
                      large trajectory count ̸= large independent sample size.              (520)


12.4     Hierarchical modelling

Many Paper III outcomes naturally contain nested sources of variation.

A generic model is:
                                yijks = β0 + β1 Mi + β2 Ej + β3 (Mi Ej )
                                                                                            (521)
                                       + uk + vs + ϵijks ,
where:


  • Mi is mechanism or architecture condition;

  • Ej is environment or task class;

  • uk is an environment-instance effect;

  • vs is an independent seed or replicate effect.


Additional levels should be included only where supported by the experimental design.

The model family should match the outcome distribution rather than assuming Gaussian errors
automatically.




                                                  137
12.5    Block I: viability and failure-time analysis

Block I contains outcomes for which ordinary means are insufficient.

For failure time
                                                   Tfail ,                                (522)

                                         S(t) = P (Tfail > t) .                           (523)

                               survival curves,       median failure time,
                        restricted mean survival time where appropriate,                  (524)
                                hazard or equivalent model contrasts.

effects should be used rather than forcing one parametric interpretation.

Recovery time

should be analysed separately from survival.

A system can survive disturbance without recovering its previous operational state.


12.6    Block I: intervention and rescue contrasts

Maintenance-node experiments should distinguish three conditions:

                                                  intact,
                                                  ablated,                                (526)
                                      ablated + specific rescue.

                               ∆rescue = E[Y | rescue] − E[Y | ablated].                  (527)


Specificity requires comparison with irrelevant rescue:

                                 ∆specific = E[Y | specific rescue]
                                                                                          (528)
                                            − E[Y | irrelevant rescue].




                                                    138
Evidence is strongest when both contrasts favour the hypothesised function.


12.7      Block II: populations are the primary replicates

Individuals within one population share ancestry, environmental history and population dynamics.
They should therefore not be treated as independent observations for population-level selection
claims.

The preferred confirmatory unit is the independently initialised replicate population.

Population-level summaries include:

                                 pt (g),      w̄t ,     Cov(wi , zi ),
                                                                                          (529)
                           lineage depth,         extinction probability.


Within-population individual data remain useful for modelling but require hierarchical or
lineage-aware treatment.


12.8      Block II: lineage dependence

Related individuals are statistically dependent because they share ancestry.

Where lineage-level outcomes are analysed, models should account for:

                                           shared ancestry,
                                             generation,
                                                                                          (530)
                                      population replicate,
                                      environmental regime.


Simple regression treating every descendant as an independent sample should be avoided.

Where practical, resampling should occur at the population or lineage level rather than at the
individual-agent level.


12.9      Block II: Price-equation quantities

                                      1                 1
                              ∆z̄ =      Cov(wi , zi ) + E [wi ∆zi ] .                    (531)
                                      w̄                w̄

                       Price decomposition ̸= causal identification by itself.            (532)


12.10    Block II: extinction is an outcome, not missing data

A population that becomes extinct under a preregistered condition has produced a scientifically
meaningful result.

Extinct replicates should not be excluded simply because later trait or performance measurements
cannot be collected.

Analyses should distinguish:

                       conditional performance among surviving populations
                                                from                                      (533)
                       overall population-level success including extinction.


Otherwise survivorship bias could make maladaptive regimes appear successful.

                                            R × F × L.                                    (534)

12.19    Auxiliary conflict analysis

The auxiliary conflict experiment should report each objective separately before considering
aggregate summaries.

Where meaningful, Pareto efficiency may be analysed, but the underlying objective vector should

                                       familiar trade-offs,
                                        novel trade-offs,
                                                                                          (559)

12.20    Non-inferiority and equivalence tests for bypasses

Failure to reject a difference is not sufficient to claim that a bypass architecture performs
equivalently.

Where the scientific hypothesis is:

                                 bypass performs comparably,                              (560)

a preregistered equivalence or non-inferiority margin should be used where possible.

Let margin be:
                                               δNI .                                      (561)

A bypass may be considered practically non-inferior when the appropriate confidence interval
excludes effects worse than:
                                              −δNI .                                      (562)

The margin must be scientifically justified before decisive testing.



                                               144
12.21     Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence

A non-significant result can arise for several reasons:

                            the mechanism has no meaningful effect,
                                        the effect is small,
                                the experiment is underpowered,                              (563)
                                     the intervention is noisy,
                            the task does not require the mechanism.


Failure to detect an effect should therefore be classified using uncertainty and power information.

Where the confidence interval remains compatible with scientifically important positive and
negative effects, the correct judgement is more likely:

                                             Suspend                                         (564)

than:
                                              Reject.                                        (565)


12.22     Power analysis

Confirmatory experiments should conduct power or precision analysis before fixing replicate
counts.

The relevant target should be the smallest effect size of scientific interest:

                                               ∆min .                                        (566)


Replicate count should be chosen to estimate or detect effects of approximately that scale with
acceptable uncertainty.

For stochastic simulations, preliminary variance estimates may be obtained from development
runs that are excluded from final confirmation.

The earlier rule:
                                              N ≥ 30                                         (567)

remains a useful default for inexpensive experiments but is not a substitute for design-specific
power analysis.


12.23     Sequential stopping

Stopping rules should be fixed before confirmatory runs.


                                                145
If sequential analysis is used, decision boundaries must account for repeated inspection of
accumulating data.

Otherwise, the experiment should use fixed replicate counts.

Stopping because the desired result has appeared is not permitted.

Likewise, continuing only because a preregistered result is negative introduces asymmetrical
researcher freedom.


12.24    Multiplicity

Paper III contains many hypotheses, architectures, task families, ablation targets and outcome
measures.

Confirmatory hypotheses should therefore be grouped into prespecified families.

Within each family, an appropriate multiplicity procedure should be defined before evaluation.

Possible strategies include:
                                   family-wise error control,
                                 false-discovery-rate control,                           (568)
                                       hierarchical testing.

The method should match the scientific role of the hypothesis family.

Exploratory analyses may remain unadjusted if clearly labelled exploratory.


12.25    Primary and secondary outcomes

Each hypothesis should nominate:


  1. one primary outcome or a small preregistered set;

  2. secondary supportive outcomes;

  3. exploratory diagnostics.


A failed primary hypothesis should not be retrospectively rescued because a secondary metric
happens to show a favourable effect.

Secondary evidence can motivate a new confirmatory experiment.


12.26    Missing and undefined outcomes

Some outcomes may become undefined.



                                               146
Examples include:

                              recovery time after irreversible failure,
                        adaptation time when threshold is never reached,                 (569)
                              calibration after complete task refusal.


Rules for such cases should be preregistered.

Undefined outcomes should not simply be removed if their occurrence depends upon experimental
condition.

Where possible, failure itself should become part of the analysis.


12.27     Outlier treatment

Exclusion of extreme runs should require an independently justified criterion such as:

                                         software failure,
                                           corrupted log,                                (570)
                               preregistered environment invalidity.


Extreme but valid biological-like or artificial behaviour is not an outlier merely because it
increases variance.

Analyses may report robust summaries alongside conventional estimates without discarding valid
runs.


12.28     Sensitivity to representation

Decisive results should be re-estimated under preregistered alternative:

                                         state encodings,
                                       temporal resolutions,
                                                                                         (571)
                                        system boundaries,
                                       evaluation horizons.


The purpose is not to demand identical numerical estimates.

Instead, robustness requires the substantive conclusion to remain intelligible under nearby
scientifically defensible choices.

Where the sign or ranking changes, the claim should be restricted.




                                                147
12.29      Sensitivity to baseline choice

Key effects should also be examined against more than one defensible baseline where Paper II
identified baseline dependence as important.

Suppose:
                                               ∆(0)                                         (572)

uses baseline M0 , while:
                                                  ′
                                               ∆(0 )                                        (573)

uses alternative baseline M′0 .

A claim whose existence depends entirely upon one convenient baseline should be restricted.


12.30      Architecture robustness

Major findings should be tested in more than one implementation where computationally feasible.

For example, retention effects might be compared across two recurrent architectures, or model-
based effects across distinct predictive-model implementations.

The scientific target is not identical numerical performance.

It is whether the mechanism-specific relation survives reasonable changes in implementation.


12.31      Environment robustness

Principal hypotheses should not depend upon one handcrafted task instance.

Where possible, decisive conclusions should be tested across:

                                  multiple environment seeds,
                                       parameter regimes,                                   (574)
                             independently generated task instances.


A mechanism that works only in one narrow environment may still be retained, but its status
should be:
                                            Restrict                                        (575)

rather than universalised.




                                               148
12.32    Leave-one-environment-family-out analysis

Where several related task families are available, one useful robustness test is to fit or summarise

For families:
                                            F1 , . . . , F k ,                                (576)

                                                  Fj .                                        (577)

12.33    Seed sensitivity

All principal results should be visualised across independent seeds rather than only through
aggregate means.

Where one or two seeds dominate the effect, report:

                                 median,       interquartile range,
                                                                                              (578)
                                   seed-level effect distribution

alongside model-based estimates where useful.

Seed heterogeneity may itself reveal unstable mechanisms.


12.34    Replication tiers

Evidence should be classified by replication level.

Tier 1: Internal replication. The effect survives new seeds and held-out environment instances
within the same implementation.

Tier 2: Implementation replication. The effect survives a meaningfully different implement-
ation of the same mechanism.

Tier 3: Task-family replication. The effect survives a different environment family requiring
the same hypothesised function.

Tier 4: Independent reproduction. The result is reproduced from released code, data or
specification by an independent implementation or research group.

Higher tiers strengthen confidence but are not assumed automatically.




                                                  149
12.35    Robustness should not be defined post hoc

Robustness analyses themselves create researcher degrees of freedom if endless variants are tried
until a preferred conclusion survives.

The principal robustness dimensions should therefore be preregistered.

Unexpected sensitivity discovered later should still be reported, but it should be labelled
exploratory.


12.36    Decision thresholds

Statistical evidence feeds into, but does not replace, the five-state decision framework.

A claim should be considered for Retain when:


  1. the preregistered primary estimand shows a scientifically meaningful effect;

  2. uncertainty excludes important null or contrary effects;

  3. the effect is mechanism-specific;

  4. serious controls do not reproduce it;

  5. key robustness checks survive.


The appropriate judgement is Restrict when the effect is robust but conditional upon:

                                      one architecture class,
                                    one environment regime,
                                                                                            (579)
                                         one representation,
                                           one timescale.


A claim should be considered for Reduce when a simpler mechanism matches the decisive effect.

The judgement should remain Suspend when uncertainty is too large to distinguish scientifically
important alternatives.

A claim should be considered for Reject when a preregistered dependency repeatedly fails under
adequately powered tests or a robust bypass defeats a claimed necessity relation.


12.37    Statistical significance cannot overrule architectural failure

A statistically strong result cannot rescue a mechanism whose interpretation fails conceptually.




                                                150
For example:
                               significant trait–fitness covariance
                                ̸⇒ causal selection mechanism,
                                                                                            (580)
                          significant confidence–accuracy correlation
                                    ̸⇒ causal self-model use.

The statistical and mechanistic requirements must both be satisfied.


12.38    Cross-block synthesis

                                               QA                                           (581)

                                              QB ,                                          (582)

                                     QB = TA→B (QA ) + ε.                                   (583)

                                  the hierarchy is significant.                             (584)

                             the experimental architecture receives

                            a vector of judgements, not one p-value.

                                           Block I: Retain,

12.42    Provisional judgement

The statistical architecture is ultimately subordinate to the scientific question.

Its role is not to manufacture a unified numerical hierarchy from heterogeneous experiments.

Instead, it should determine how strongly the data support each explicitly stated dependency.

                           estimate the causal or functional contrast
                             that the hypothesis actually predicts;

                                                                                        (588)
                                         test robustness;
                            and allow different parts of the hierarchy

13     Preregistered Decision Framework

The experimental programme requires decisions that are fixed before decisive results are known.
Without such rules, an apparent failure could be reinterpreted after the fact as partial success,
while a favourable result could be promoted beyond the level actually tested.

Paper III therefore assigns every major mechanism and transition one of five possible outcomes:

                  Retain,     Restrict,     Reduce,         Suspend,    Reject.             (589)


These outcomes apply to specified claims, not to whole domains such as “evolution”, “intelligence”
or “selfhood”.

A block may therefore contain several different verdicts.


13.1    Decision states

Retain means that the preregistered claim survives its primary mechanism-specific tests, serious
matched controls, relevant robustness checks and any required intervention or rescue criteria
within the stated scope.

Restrict means that the claim survives, but only under a narrower range of architectures,
environments, representations, timescales or causal conditions than originally proposed.

Reduce means that the observed effect is reproducible but does not require the nominated
mechanism because a simpler or already established mechanism explains the decisive evidence
equally well.

Suspend means that the available experiment cannot distinguish among scientifically important
alternatives with adequate precision or causal specificity.

Reject means that the preregistered dependency repeatedly fails under adequately informative
tests, or that a robust counterexample defeats a claim of necessity.

The distinction between these outcomes is important:

                            Restrict ̸= Reduce ̸= Suspend ̸= Reject.                        (590)


A narrow but real mechanism should not be rejected merely because it is not universal. Likewise,
an underpowered experiment should not be interpreted as evidence against the mechanism.


13.2    Claims are judged at the level tested

Evidence does not automatically propagate upwards.




                                               154
If an experiment establishes:
                                                M
                                             A −−→ B                                       (591)

under specified conditions, it does not establish:

                                                N
                                            B −−→ C.                                       (592)


Nor does success in an artificial implementation establish that the same mechanism is naturally
necessary.

The decision framework therefore attaches each verdict to:

                                        a defined capacity,
                                    a nominated mechanism,

  5. the primary estimand;

  6. the minimum effect or equivalence margin of scientific interest;

  7. the required intervention, ablation or rescue where applicable;

  8. the principal robustness checks;

  9. the bypass architecture or alternative explanation;

 10. the rule assigning Retain, Restrict, Reduce, Suspend or Reject.

13.11     Mechanism-level decision rules

A mechanism should receive Retain only when all of the following are satisfied:


  1. the primary preregistered effect is present at scientifically meaningful magnitude;

  2. uncertainty is sufficiently narrow to exclude important contrary interpretations;

  3. the effect is selective for conditions where the mechanism should matter;

  4. targeted intervention behaves as predicted where intervention is technically possible;

  5. matched alternatives do not reproduce the decisive effect;

  6. preregistered robustness checks do not overturn the interpretation.


Failure of one requirement does not always imply rejection.

For example, lack of implementation replication may justify:

                                            Restrict                                        (601)


                                               162
rather than:
                                                Reject.                               (602)


13.12      Necessary-dependency claims face a higher threshold

Necessity claims require more than showing that mechanism M helps produce capacity B.

Evidence that:
                                              M −→ B                                  (603)

under one architecture establishes at most a route of synthetic sufficiency.

A necessary-dependency claim additionally requires serious attempts to produce:

                                                  B                                   (604)

without:
                                                  M.                                  (605)

The relevant adversarial target is:
                                              ¬M ∧ B.                                 (606)

A robust bypass defeats universal necessity even when M remains useful.


13.13      Sufficiency claims remain architecture-bound

Artificial sufficiency should be stated at the narrowest justified level.

A result of the form:
                                            A + M −→ B                                (607)

supports:
                                       M can contribute to B
                                      within architecture class A                     (608)
                                   under the tested conditions.

It does not justify:
                                   M universally generates B.                         (609)

Any later results report should preserve this architecture-bound wording in its Results and
Conclusion.




                                                 163
13.14    Gate logic is replaced by dependency logic

The previous experimental design used phase gates that could be read as:

             Phase I passes −→ Phase II becomes legitimate −→ Phase III follows.       (610)


That logic is no longer appropriate.

The revised programme asks instead:

                            Is the later question logically dependent
                                                                                       (611)
                                       upon the earlier result?


If yes, the earlier failure constrains how the later experiment may be interpreted.

If no, the later experiment may proceed independently.


13.15    Three progression cases

Three cases should be distinguished.

Case A: strict experimental dependence.

Suppose a later experiment requires the actual output of an earlier mechanism:

                                               A→
                                                − B.                                   (612)


If A cannot be instantiated or measured adequately, the dependent test of B may need to be:

                                              Suspend.                                 (613)


Case B: theoretical dependence but independent construction.

A later capacity can be engineered directly even though the proposed natural dependency from
an earlier capacity has failed.

In that case, the later experiment may proceed, but it tests:

                                        synthetic sufficiency                          (614)

rather than confirming the failed transition.

Case C: logical independence.

The later question does not require the earlier mechanism.

The experiment should proceed normally.


                                                 164
This distinction prevents failure in one part of the architecture from artificially terminating
unrelated questions.


13.16    Examples of non-blocking failures

Several important failures should not stop later work.

If reciprocal organisational closure is rejected as necessary for self-maintenance, Block II may
still test reproduction and heredity in artificial agents constructed by another route.

If explicit retention is unnecessary for some tasks, Block IV may still test world-model use.

If model-based planning is reduced to a powerful model-free mechanism, Block V may still ask
whether the resulting agent uses self-related information.

If reflective self-modelling is rejected as necessary for flexible agency, the auxiliary conflict
experiment may still be performed.

Thus:
                  failure of one arrow ̸= automatic failure of every later node.            (615)


13.17    Examples of interpretation-changing failures

Some failures do alter later interpretation.

If Block II cannot establish heredity, then observed population change must not later be described
as selection on inherited variation.

If Block III fails to demonstrate experience-dependent policy modification, later performance
should not be described as arising from learning unless learning is independently re-established
in the later architecture.

If Block IV fails to show causal world-model use, Block V should not describe the tested agent
as a model-based reasoner merely because a predictive module is present.

The programme may continue, but its terminology must follow the surviving evidence.


13.18    Decision inheritance is downward only

A failed or restricted lower-level claim can constrain how dependent higher claims are worded.

Evidence does not travel in the opposite direction.

For example, successful Block IV performance cannot retrospectively establish that Block III
retention or learning was necessary.

Likewise, successful Block V self-monitoring cannot prove that model-based planning caused the
development of self-modelling.


                                               165
Therefore:
                                   later success cannot rescue
                                                                                            (616)
                                  an earlier failed dependency.

13.21      Quarantine rule

Claims that are suspended, untested or supported only by exploratory evidence remain outside
the active architecture.

They may be retained in a quarantine ledger with:

                                     current evidential status,
                                      reason for quarantine,
                                                                                                (618)
                                  decisive evidence still required,
                                  conditions for reconsideration.


The quarantine ledger is not a list of discarded ideas.

It is a mechanism for preventing unresolved claims from entering later arguments as though they
had already been established.


13.22      Decision revisions require new evidence

Verdicts may change as evidence changes.

A previously rejected claim can be reconsidered if:

                           a materially different mechanism is proposed,
                                 a failed assumption is corrected,                              (619)
                       new evidence defeats the original counterexample.


Likewise, a retained claim can later be restricted or rejected by stronger experiments.

Revision is therefore permitted, but not through reinterpretation alone.

A decision changes because the evidence changes.

13.23     No privileged outcome

The preregistration must not define theoretical confirmation as the preferred result.

For every principal claim:

                       Retain,    Restrict,   Reduce,   Suspend,      Reject              (620)

must be treated as legitimate scientific outcomes.

A programme in which only Retain counts as success would create pressure to reinterpret
ambiguous evidence in favour of the hierarchy.

Paper III instead defines success as obtaining a justified verdict.


13.24     Final decision record

After all confirmatory analyses are complete, the project should publish a machine-readable and
human-readable decision record.

For each hypothesis, the record should contain:

  1. hypothesis identifier;

  2. preregistered wording;

  3. primary estimand;

  4. primary result;

  5. uncertainty interval;

  6. intervention result where applicable;

  7. bypass result;

  8. robustness summary;

  9. final decision;

 10. scope of that decision;

 11. any unresolved qualification.

This record should preserve failed and mixed results alongside retained ones.


13.25     Provisional judgement

The purpose of the decision framework is not to force the experimental programme through five
stages.

It is to determine which proposed relations survive.

The final architecture may therefore be:

                                               a chain,                                   (621)

a branching structure,
                                             a network,                                   (622)

or a collection of partly independent mechanisms.

The experiment should allow the evidence to decide.

The governing rule is:

                  do not preserve a transition because later sections need it;

claims, then extend the infrastructure only when a later test requires additional machinery.

The implementation sequence is consequently practical rather than ontological:

                                       implementation order

Building Block I before Block II is an efficient engineering decision. It does not imply that
self-maintenance necessarily generates reproduction.

14.29    Minimum viable research programme

If resources permit only a small first study, Paper III does not require all five blocks to be

  2. reproduce the driven Gray–Scott baseline;

  4. preregister one maintenance-edge hypothesis;

  5. perform targeted ablation;

  6. perform specific and irrelevant rescue;

  7. run the matched nulls;

  8. publish the raw trajectories and decision record.

14.30    Criteria for extending the implementation

A later implementation stage should be added when at least one of the following applies:

  1. the next block tests an unresolved high-priority dependency;

  2. an earlier result generates a specific new discriminating prediction;

  3. a bypass requires a new architecture;

  4. replication requires a different implementation;

  5. a robustness test requires a broader environment family.

Expansion should not occur merely because the next block appears later in the paper.

15      What the Experiment Cannot Establish

Paper III is intentionally ambitious in experimental design and conservative in interpretation.
Even if every principal experiment succeeds, the results would establish only a set of controlled
functional, causal and dependency relations within specified artificial systems.

                                                 187
The experiment does not test everything represented in the wider Theory of Everything research
programme, nor can artificial construction by itself settle questions about natural history,
ontology, life, intelligence or phenomenal consciousness.

The governing limitation is:

                                  successful artificial construction
                                                                                          (675)
                           ̸= demonstration of universal natural necessity.


15.1    Synthetic sufficiency is not natural necessity

Suppose artificial architecture A, supplemented by mechanism M , reliably produces capacity B:

                                           A + M −→ B.                                    (676)


The strongest immediate conclusion is that M can contribute to B within the tested architecture
and environment.

This does not establish:
                                  M is universally necessary for B,                       (677)

nor:
                     M is the route by which natural systems produce B.                   (678)

A necessity claim requires serious attempts to construct:

                                              ¬M ∧ B.                                     (679)


Even failure to find such a bypass in Paper III would establish necessity only within the tested
architecture classes, not across every physically possible system.


15.2    Artificial reconstruction does not recover historical origin

A successful experiment can demonstrate that a transition is possible under specified artificial
conditions.




                                                 188
It cannot, by itself, show that the same sequence occurred historically in the origin of:

                                                life,
                                             heredity,
                                      Darwinian evolution,
                                                                                            (680)
                                             learning,
                                           intelligence,
                                       reflective cognition.


Historical origin claims require independent evidence from the relevant natural sciences.

Accordingly,
                      synthetic reconstruction ̸= historical reconstruction.                (681)


15.3    Block ordering does not establish an evolutionary ladder

The five-block ordering is an experimental organisation of questions.

It does not establish that natural systems must develop through:

                 maintenance → heredity → learning → agency → self-modelling                (682)

in that order.

Later blocks may be implementable independently of earlier ones.

Bypass architectures may reveal:
                                              A ̸→ B                                        (683)

even when both capacities exist.

The final dependency structure may therefore be a chain, a branching graph, a network of partly
independent mechanisms or no coherent hierarchy at all.


15.4    The experiment cannot establish a universal measure of organisation

Paper II rejected the original proposal that heterogeneous constraint quantities should be
interpreted as independent coordinates of one universal cross-scale vector.

Paper III therefore does not establish:
                                              Γtotal ,                                      (684)

a universal scalar of organisation, complexity, intelligence or constraint.

The typed profile
                                          C = [D, F, T, O]                                  (685)


                                                189
is a comparative organisational device, not a discovered fundamental state variable.

Consequently,

        successful measurement across blocks ̸= discovery of one universal quantity.    (686)


15.5    Artificial self-maintenance does not establish life

Block I may demonstrate that an artificial system contributes causally to maintaining the
organisation required for its continued operation.

Such a result would not determine whether the system is alive.

Life may involve additional properties such as:

                                             metabolism,
                                  autonomous material organisation,
                                            reproduction,                               (687)
                                         open-ended heredity,
                                      evolutionary individuality.


The experiment can isolate formal properties associated with living systems without resolving
the general definition of life.

                                       self-maintenance ̸⇒ life.                        (688)

16     Relationship to the Minimal Principles and Constraint Ar-
       chitectures

Paper III is the experimental component of a three-paper research architecture. Its role can be
understood only by distinguishing what the preceding papers propose from what the present
experiments are capable of testing.

The three papers perform different scientific tasks:

                         Paper I: identify candidate conditions, mechanisms
                                     and unresolved transitions;

                          Paper II: test whether constraint concepts admit
                                                                                          (721)
                               mathematically defensible comparison;

                           Paper III: expose selected dependency claims
                                to controlled artificial intervention.


None of the three papers is intended to confirm the other two by definition. Their relationship
is therefore one of constrained interaction rather than mutual presupposition.




                                                 198
16.1    Paper I provides the candidate dependency architecture

The Minimal Principles Paper asks which claims deserve a place in a research architecture
extending from physical dynamics through organisation, evolution and intelligence towards the
unresolved problem of consciousness.

Its adversarial review produced several different evidential categories rather than one continuous
hierarchy:
                  FC1 : dynamical state evolution and physical admissibility,
                             EC1 : sustained non-equilibrium driving,
                             EM1 : causally consequential retention,                        (722)
                       EM2 : natural selection in heritable populations,
               EM3 : closed-loop regulation, learning and model-based control.

It also retained four unresolved transition problems:

                      TP1 : dissipative organisation → self-maintenance,
                       TP2 : self-maintenance → Darwinian population,
                                                                                            (723)
                        TP3 : adaptive regulation → intelligent agency,
                TP4 : intelligent agency → reflective self-modelled intelligence.


Phenomenal consciousness was not converted into another transition mechanism. It remained:

                    OP1 : The Physical Basis of Phenomenal Consciousness.                   (724)


Paper III inherits these distinctions as questions to test, not propositions to demonstrate.


16.2    Paper III does not experimentally reproduce all of Paper I

Not every component of the Minimal Principles architecture has a corresponding artificial
experiment.

Framework Condition 1 concerns physical admissibility at a level more general than the artificial
systems constructed here. Paper III presupposes ordinary lawful computation and simulation;
it does not experimentally test whether reality itself must obey a particular fundamental
state-evolution principle.

Likewise, sustained non-equilibrium driving appears in the Gray–Scott baseline and maintenance
experiments, but Paper III does not attempt to establish EC1 as a universal condition for every
possible form of organisation.




                                               199
The relationship is therefore selective:

                                        Paper I architecture
                                                  ⊃                                     (725)
                         subset accessible to controlled artificial testing.


Experimental tractability should not be mistaken for theoretical priority.


16.3    Block I tests part of Transition Problem 1

Experimental Block I corresponds most directly to:

                 TP1 : dissipative organisation → self-maintaining organisation.        (726)


The Gray–Scott baseline first demonstrates why persistent pattern formation is insufficient.
Externally driven dynamics can sustain organised structure without establishing that the
structure causally maintains itself.

The subsequent maintenance architectures then distinguish:

                                         passive persistence,
                                        external maintenance,
                                                                                        (727)
                               internal feed-forward maintenance,
                                 reciprocal internal maintenance.


A successful result would therefore narrow TP1 by demonstrating at least one artificial route
from persistence towards internally generated maintenance.

It would not solve the historical origin of biological self-maintenance.


16.4    Block II separates Transition Problem 2 from Effective Mechanism 2

Paper I distinguishes the emergence of a Darwinian population from the later operation of
natural selection within such a population.

Paper III preserves that distinction.

Block II first asks:
                                           What reproduces?                             (728)




                                                 200
It then tests:
                                          individual identity,
                                             reproduction,
                                                                                               (729)
                                    parent–offspring transmission,
                                          heritable variation.

Only after those relations are established does the experiment test:

                           EM2 : natural selection in a heritable population.                  (730)


This ordering prevents selection from being treated as the generative principle that creates the
very heredity and reproduction required for selection to operate.


16.5     Block III connects Effective Mechanism 1 with selected components of
         Effective Mechanism 3

Paper I treats causally consequential retention as Effective Mechanism 1 and places closed-loop
regulation, learning and model-based control within Effective Mechanism 3.

Block III therefore does not decompose EM3 into retention, feedback and learning. Instead, it
brings one experimentally tractable implementation of EM1 into direct comparison with selected
components of EM3.

The principal factorial design is:
                                              R × F × L,                                       (731)

where:
                                 R = policy-accessible retained state,
                               F = within-episode closed-loop feedback,                        (732)
                       L = between-episode experience-dependent learning.

This notation is experimental rather than ontological. Learning itself retains the effects of earlier
experience in changed policy parameters, so R is not intended to represent every possible form
of historical influence.

The experiment instead asks whether three differently located causal channels make distinguish-
able contributions:

                            retained state changes which past distinctions
                                remain directly available to the policy;
                                                                                               (733)
                    feedback changes action from newly arriving information;
                       learning changes the policy used in future episodes.


If these distinctions collapse under matched intervention, the corresponding architecture in

                                                  201
Paper I should be revised.

If they remain experimentally separable, the result supports functional distinctions among EM1
and the tested components of EM3 without establishing a necessary hierarchy among them.


16.6    Block IV addresses Transition Problem 3

Transition Problem 3 asks what, if anything, separates successful adaptive regulation from more
general intelligent agency.

Paper III operationalises one candidate distinction through:

                                   structural generalisation.                             (734)


The decisive tests move beyond ordinary held-out examples towards:

                                      novel composition,
                                    altered causal relations,
                                     delayed consequences,                                (735)
                                    hidden-state inference,
                                     new problem families.


World models and counterfactual rollout are treated as candidate mechanisms, not definitional
requirements for intelligence.

Consequently, Paper III can discover either:

                   world-model use contributes to structural generalisation,              (736)

or:
                 structural generalisation can bypass explicit world-model use.           (737)

Either result modifies TP3 constructively.


16.7    Block V addresses Transition Problem 4

Transition Problem 4 concerns the move from intelligent agency to reflective self-modelled
intelligence.

Block V gives that transition an intervention-sensitive interpretation.

The target is not merely:
                                        self-description,                                 (738)




                                               202
nor:
                                       confidence prediction.                               (739)

The stronger candidate relation is:

                        representation of a property of the system itself
                                                                                            (740)
                         −→ causal modification of subsequent control.


Self-model misinformation and correction provide the key intervention–rescue test.

A positive result would support one functional route to reflective self-modelling. A successful
bypass would show that some apparently reflective capacities can be realised without the proposed
endogenous representation.


16.8    Open Problem 1 has no Paper III block

The absence of a consciousness block is theoretically significant.

Paper I concluded that the present architecture provides no validated bridge from

                               reflective self-modelled intelligence                        (741)

to
                                   phenomenal consciousness.                                (742)

Paper III therefore stops its principal experimental sequence at Block V.

There is no:
                                      Block VI: Consciousness.                              (743)

Such a block would create the appearance that phenomenal experience is simply the next
experimentally defined capacity in the hierarchy, when no justified operational transition has
been established.


16.9    The auxiliary conflict test does not restore a consciousness bridge

The conflict-representation experiment is intentionally outside Blocks I–V.

Its purpose is to test whether explicit representation of competing objectives provides functional
value beyond:
                                  scalar reward optimisation,
                                ordinary multi-objective control,                           (744)
                                      model-based prediction.



                                                203

                       functional value of explicit conflict representation.            (745)

16.10     Paper II changes what Paper III is allowed to measure

The Mathematics of Constraint Paper began with a candidate cross-scale vector:

                          Γ = (Γstate , Γtrans , Γpath , ΠC , ϕK , χint , R(τ )) .      (746)

The proposed quantities differ in mathematical role, dependence upon baseline, representation,
timescale and explanandum. Some are nested; others describe different kinds of functional or
historical relation.

Paper III therefore does not attempt to validate:

                                                    Γ                                   (747)

as a universal state variable.

Instead, it inherits the weaker typed profile:

                                           C = [D, F, T, O].                            (748)

16.15     The three papers must not form a circular confirmation system

A serious risk in a multi-paper programme is evidential circularity.

Paper I could propose a mechanism because it appears compatible with Paper II; Paper II
could choose measurements because they fit Paper I; Paper III could then engineer the proposed
mechanism and claim experimental confirmation of the original architecture.

That sequence would be scientifically weak.

The programme therefore requires:

                            Paper I supplies hypotheses, not answers;
                Paper II supplies admissibility tests, not validation by notation;       (762)
                 Paper III supplies interventions, not engineered confirmation.


A hypothesis must remain capable of failing at every stage.


16.16     Evidence can flow back into Paper I

Although the evidential structure must not be circular, it is iterative.

Results from Paper III should modify Paper I when they directly bear upon its dependency
claims.

For example, a robust artificial bypass of:

                                              A −→ B                                     (763)

would provide evidence against treating A as universally necessary for B.

Depending upon scope, Paper I should then:

                               Restrict,      Reduce,     or Reject                      (764)

the corresponding dependency claim.

The architecture is therefore revisable.

It is not a fixed hierarchy awaiting experimental illustration.




                                                 207
16.17    Evidence can also flow back into Paper II

Paper III may reveal that a candidate measurement is:

                             unstable under representation change,
                              redundant with another descriptor,
                                                                                        (765)
                               insensitive to causal intervention,
                             useful only within a narrow domain.


Such results should feed back into the Mathematics of Constraint programme.

A descriptor retained provisionally in Paper II can therefore later be:

                                           restricted,
                                           redefined,
                                                                                        (766)
                                           replaced,
                                           removed.


Mathematical notation does not protect a quantity from empirical failure.


16.18    The research loop is iterative rather than circular

The legitimate interaction among the papers can be represented as:

                                 Paper I: propose dependency
                                               ↓
                     Paper II: clarify measurable mathematical structure
                                               ↓
                              Paper III: intervene experimentally                       (767)
                                               ↓
                                revise Paper I and/or Paper II
                                               ↓
                              generate a sharper new hypothesis.


The crucial difference from circular confirmation is that every return step must be driven by
new evidence or a demonstrated logical correction.

Nothing is preserved merely to maintain coherence among the papers.

17    Open-Science Requirements

The adversarial value of Paper III depends upon other researchers being able to reconstruct not
only the reported results but the chain of decisions that produced them. Releasing a final code
repository is therefore insufficient. The experiment should preserve hypotheses, configurations,
interventions, failed runs, bypass architectures, raw trajectories, analysis scripts and decision
records in forms that permit independent inspection and reproduction.

These requirements are consistent with the broader principle that scientific data and metadata
should remain findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable where practicable [11].

The central requirement is:

                   claim −→ preregistered specification −→ implementation
                                                                                           (782)
                              −→ raw result −→ analysis −→ decision

with every transition in that chain recoverable.

Open science is therefore not treated as an administrative appendix to the experiment. It is one
of the controls against post-hoc theoretical reconstruction.




                                                212
17.1     The reproducibility target

A complete release should allow an independent researcher to answer at least five questions.


  1. What exactly was hypothesised before the decisive experiment?

  2. Which code, environment and architecture instantiated the test?

  3. Which raw observations produced the reported statistics?

  4. Which analysis transformed those observations into the published result?

  5. Why did that result receive its final Retain/Restrict/Reduce/Suspend/Reject judgement?


If one of these questions cannot be answered from the archived materials, the evidential chain is
incomplete.


17.2     Preregistration archive

Every confirmatory hypothesis should have a preregistration record created before decisive
evaluation.

The record should contain:

                        hypothesis wording,          operational definitions,
                             architectures,        environment classes,
                        primary estimand,          sample or replicate plan,               (783)
                                interventions,          bypass tests,
                             robustness analyses,           decision rules.


The preregistration should be timestamped and preserved in an immutable or versioned repository.

Later clarification is permitted, but it must not overwrite the original record.


17.3     Amendment ledger

Changes made after preregistration should be recorded explicitly.

For amendment Aj , store:
                                       Aj = (tj , rj , cj , ej ) ,                         (784)

where:


  • tj is the time of the amendment;

  • rj is the reason for the change;

                                                  213
   • cj records the changed specification;

   • ej records whether decisive data had already been inspected.

A correction made before decisive results are visible differs epistemically from a change introduced
after the preferred result has failed.

Both may be legitimate, but they should remain distinguishable.


17.4    Machine-readable experiment manifests

Each confirmatory experiment should have a machine-readable manifest linked to its preregistra-
tion.

The manifest should include:

                               experiment ID,          hypothesis IDs,
                        architecture versions,         environment versions,
                        training budget,        seed list or seed procedure,
                                                                                              (785)
                         held-out evaluation suite,        intervention IDs,
                                   rescue IDs,         bypass IDs,
                            primary analysis script,        decision rule.


Human-readable prose explains the science.

The manifest records exactly what the computer executed.

Both are required.


17.5    Versioned source code

All code used for confirmatory experiments should be version controlled.

Each published result should identify:

                                         source-code commit,
                                          repository release,
                                                                                              (786)
                                    dependency specification,
                                         runtime environment.


Where practical, the release should include a reproducible software environment through a lock
file, container specification or equivalent mechanism.

The purpose is not merely convenience. Software dependencies can change numerical behaviour
and therefore belong to the experimental provenance.

                                                 214
17.6     Configuration hashes

Configuration files should be treated as experimental objects.

For configuration C, record cryptographic digest:

                                                   hC = H(C).                               (787)


Equivalent hashes should be recorded for:

                                       architecture definitions,
                                      environment definitions,
                                                                                            (788)
                                            held-out test suites,
                                     intervention specifications.


This allows the published result to be tied to an exact experimental specification even when the
repository later evolves.


17.7     Raw trajectories are primary records

The primary empirical record should be the raw experimental trajectory, not the final summary
table.

For agent-based experiments, preserve where applicable:

                                     Ot ,          At ,         Xt ,   Yt ,
                              Mt ,          θt ,          intervention status,              (789)
                environment state,           architecture state accessible to analysis.


Block I should additionally preserve component states and maintenance relations.

Block II requires population and lineage records.

Blocks IV and V require the internal states needed to reproduce model and self-model intervention
analyses.

If storage limits make complete preservation impossible, the retention policy should be fixed
before confirmatory runs rather than after inspecting which trajectories are interesting.


17.8     Lineage records for Block II

Population experiments require especially careful archival structure because aggregate trait
frequencies cannot reconstruct ancestry.



                                                          215
The lineage record should include:

                                      individual identifier,

                                 transmissible parental state,
                                                                                          (790)
                                 offspring transmissible state,

                                     reproductive success,
                                     environmental regime.

                                                 Glin                                     (791)

                          architecture ID,        training configuration,
                                training seed,          training step,                    (792)

17.10    Intervention registry

Every confirmatory ablation, corruption, perturbation and rescue should appear in a public

                                          target mechanism,

                                      matched-damage control,
                                          rescue operation,

17.11     Bypass registry

Bypass architectures deserve the same archival status as preferred architectures.

A successful bypass should be preserved prominently rather than treated as a failed version of

17.21    Negative results are first-class outputs

hierarchy.

                                                                                             (808)

17.30        Independent reproduction package

The strongest release should include a minimal reproduction package requiring no knowledge of

   1. a frozen confirmatory manifest;

   4. instructions for reproducing one principal analysis;

   6. instructions for regenerating one published figure or table.

17.31      Reproduction and replication should be distinguished

Two forms of confirmation should remain separate.

Computational reproduction asks whether the same code, data and specification regenerate
the reported result.

Scientific replication asks whether the same claim survives new runs, new implementations or
new task families.

Thus:
                         reproducible calculation ̸= replicated mechanism.                    (817)

Paper III requires both where feasible, but they answer different questions.


17.32      Replication tiers

Internal activations or representations should not be released only for runs that support the

                                           successful runs,
                                             failed runs,
                                                                                            (819)
                                      control architectures,
                                      bypass architectures.

Otherwise mechanistic interpretation could be biased by selective inspectability.

                                                                                          (822)

Very large artefacts may require tiered storage, but metadata describing them should remain

The archival aim is that a future researcher can understand not only what the project concluded

17.37      No deletion of superseded theory

When the dependency architecture changes, earlier versions should remain archived.

A rejected hypothesis should not disappear from the historical record.

should remain traceable.

This is especially important for a programme whose explicit purpose is to remove unsupported

18        Possible Outcome Space

Paper III is not designed around one preferred empirical story. The experiments may support
parts of the proposed dependency architecture, weaken others, identify alternative routes, collapse
apparently distinct mechanisms into simpler explanations or fail to reveal any coherent hierarchy
at all.

The possible outcome space is therefore broader than:

                                      hypothesis confirmed                                   (828)

or:
                                       hypothesis rejected.                                  (829)

The central aim is to discover which dependency structure is best supported after matched
controls, interventions, rescues, bypass searches and robustness tests have been completed.



                                               230
The governing principle is:

                              the experiment does not ask whether
                        the proposed hierarchy can be made to appear;
                                                                                    (830)
                         it asks what dependency architecture remains
                after the hierarchy has been given serious opportunities to fail.


18.1    Outcome 1: substantial support for the proposed architecture

One possible result is that many of the candidate dependencies survive.

For example:
                                     internal maintenance
                                                ↓
                               heritable reproducing population
                                                ↓
                                retention, feedback and learning                    (831)
                                                ↓
                                structurally generalising agency
                                                ↓
                              reflective self-modelled intelligence.

Such a result would be important because the transitions would have survived serious null
architectures, selective interventions and bypass searches.

Even then, the conclusion would remain conditional.

Paper III would support:

               one experimentally validated artificial dependency architecture,     (832)

not:
                   the unique universal architecture of life and intelligence.      (833)


18.2    Outcome 2: a partial hierarchy

A more likely outcome may be that some transitions survive while others do not.




                                               231
For example:
                                  self-maintenance: Retain,
                                         heredity: Retain,
                                      selection: Retain,
                                      retention: Retain,
                                                                                        (834)
                                      feedback: Retain,
                                      learning: Restrict,
                                world-model necessity: Reject,
                              reflective self-model use: Suspend.

This would not represent a failed Theory of Everything programme.

It would show that the original architecture contained both robust and unnecessary structure.

The revised dependency map should then preserve only the surviving relations.


18.3    Outcome 3: branching dependencies

The experiments may reveal that one lower-level capacity supports several higher-level routes
without determining a single sequence.

For example:
                         retention −→ learning −→ adaptive agency,
                                                                                        (835)
                     retention −→ model-free control −→ adaptive agency.

This would indicate that retention can contribute to more than one route towards adaptive
agency, rather than functioning as one step in a mandatory linear progression.

A branching result would therefore weaken any claim that the system must develop through a
single ordered ladder. The appropriate response would be to replace the linear transition map
with a branching dependency structure whose separate routes are tested independently.


18.4    Outcome 4: convergent routes to the same capacity

Different mechanisms may independently generate similar higher-level capabilities.

Suppose both:
                                            M1 −→ B                                     (836)

and:
                                            M2 −→ B                                     (837)

survive serious tests.




                                               232
The correct conclusion would be:

                           capacity B admits multiple sufficient routes;
                                                                                      (838)
                             neither route is automatically necessary.


This possibility is especially relevant to:

                                         self-maintenance,
                                     structural generalisation,                       (839)
                                         reflective control.


A Theory of Everything architecture that permits multiple realisation may be more accurate
than one built around a unique mechanism.


18.5    Outcome 5: successful bypass of a proposed necessity

A particularly informative outcome would be a robust system satisfying:

                                              ¬M ∧ B,                                 (840)

where M had been proposed as necessary for capacity B.

Examples include:

                           self-maintenance without reciprocal closure,
                 learning without persistent recurrent memory during execution,
                                                                                      (841)
                 structural generalisation without explicit world-model rollout,
                   reflective control without an endogenous confidence model.


Such a bypass would be a major result.

It would not show that the bypassed mechanism is useless.

It would show:
                               M is not universally necessary for B                   (842)

within the scope reached by the experiment.


18.6    Outcome 6: reduction to a simpler mechanism

Some higher-level explanatory constructs may turn out to add no independent causal value.




                                                233
For example:
                          reciprocal closure → ordinary causal dependency,
                    explicit conflict representation → multi-objective control,          (843)
                      self-modelling → task-specific uncertainty estimation.

Where a simpler mechanism reproduces the decisive effects, the appropriate verdict is:

                                               Reduce.                                   (844)


Reduction is not a lesser scientific result.

It identifies where the research architecture contains explanatory machinery that the evidence
does not require.


18.7    Outcome 7: architecture-dependent mechanisms

A mechanism may work reliably but only within a narrow implementation class.

For example, world-model rollout may provide a clear advantage in one planning architecture
but no benefit in another.

Likewise, reciprocal maintenance may matter only when:

                                     component failure is local,
                                  repair resources are constrained,                      (845)
                       disturbance timescales fall within a specific range.


The correct response is:
                                               Restrict,                                 (846)

not to discard the mechanism or universalise it.

Such results help identify the actual domain of validity of each proposed dependency.


18.8    Outcome 8: environment-dependent mechanisms

Some effects may be driven more strongly by environment structure than by the intrinsic
hierarchy of the agent.

For example:

                 retention matters only when present observation is insufficient,
                    feedback matters only under unpredictable disturbance,
                                                                                         (847)
                           learning matters only when mappings change,
               planning matters only when consequences are delayed or branching.

                                                 234
This would support an ecological interpretation:

                      mechanism value depends upon problem structure.                    (848)


The hierarchy would then be better understood as a set of conditional capabilities than as an
intrinsic universal ranking.


18.9    Outcome 9: measurement-sensitive results

Paper II predicts that some candidate measures may depend strongly upon:

                                             baseline,
                                        representation,
                                                                                         (849)
                                             timescale,
                                      system boundary.


Paper III may confirm that sensitivity experimentally.

A result that changes sign, ranking or interpretation under nearby defensible measurement
choices should not be described as robust.

The appropriate verdict may be:
                                             Restrict                                    (850)

or:
                                             Suspend.                                    (851)

Such an outcome would feed directly back into Paper II.


18.10    Outcome 10: descriptor without mechanism

A quantity may predict behaviour without identifying its cause.

For example:
                                             R(τ ) > 0                                   (852)

may predict future performance while:

                                        Rcausal (∆) ≈ 0.                                 (853)


Likewise, confidence may correlate strongly with accuracy while manipulation of the confidence
representation produces no control change.




                                                235
The correct distinction is:

                              predictive descriptor ̸= causal mechanism.                   (854)


Paper III should preserve useful descriptors even when their mechanistic interpretation fails.


18.11    Outcome 11: mechanism without broad generalisation

The reverse outcome is also possible.

A mechanism may have a clear causal effect while remaining narrow.

For example:
                      world-model corruption −→ specific planning deficit                  (855)

may be reproducible even though the system fails on novel problem families.

This would support:
                                          causal model use                                 (856)

while rejecting:
                                   broad structural generalisation.                        (857)

Mechanism validation and capability breadth should therefore remain separate.


18.12    Outcome 12: no clean distinction between candidate mechanisms

Some mechanisms may prove experimentally inseparable.

For example, retention and learning may be so deeply coupled in a particular architecture that
selective intervention cannot remove one without damaging the other.

The correct outcome would be:
                                              Suspend,                                     (858)

unless a new experimental design resolves the confounding.

Paper III should not force a mechanistic distinction simply because the conceptual framework
contains one.




                                                 236
18.13    Outcome 13: collapse of the proposed hierarchy

A more radical result would occur if:

                       later capacities can be implemented independently,
                            bypass architectures repeatedly succeed,                     (859)
                      earlier mechanisms provide no necessary contribution.


The original hierarchy could then collapse into:

                  a collection of largely independent functional mechanisms.             (860)


This would be a major theoretical result.

It would show that the appearance of a hierarchy came from conceptual organisation rather
than underlying causal dependence.


18.14    Outcome 14: emergence of a different hierarchy

The experiments may reveal a dependency architecture not anticipated by the original programme.

For example:
                                     retention −→ learning,
                                  learning −→ self-monitoring,                           (861)
                          self-monitoring −→ structural generalisation
might receive stronger support than the expected ordering.

Such a result should not be forced back into the original transition map.

Instead, Paper I should be revised around the new dependency structure.


18.15    Outcome 15: no coherent artificial hierarchy

The most adversarial possibility is that no stable dependency hierarchy survives.

Results might show:
                                strong architecture dependence,
                                        multiple bypasses,
                                   weak cross-block relations,                           (862)
                                 large representation sensitivity,
                             no reproducible monotonic progression.




                                               237
The conclusion would then be:

                              the tested capacities do not form one
                                                                                             (863)
                          experimentally supported artificial hierarchy.


That would be a highly informative result.

It would prevent the wider Theory of Everything programme from importing a hierarchy that
the evidence does not support.


18.16      Outcome 16: unexpectedly strong cross-block relations

The opposite result is also possible.

Distinct block-level quantities may exhibit reproducible relations that were not assumed in
advance.

For example:
                                        QB = TA→B (QA ) + ε                                  (864)

may hold across architectures and task families.

Such a relation would be scientifically interesting, particularly if:

                                 it survives held-out prediction,
                                  it is robust to representation,                            (865)
                           it has intervention-sensitive interpretation.


The result should first be treated as a new candidate relation.

Exploratory discovery does not become confirmation until independently tested.


18.17      Outcome 17: candidate cross-scale invariant

A particularly important long-term outcome would be evidence that one relation identified in
artificial systems transports to a natural domain.

Suppose:
                                         QB = TA→B (QA ).                                    (866)

is validated artificially and later predicts a biological relation under a preregistered mapping.

This could provide the first serious evidence for a deeper cross-scale regularity.

Such a result would be relevant to the Stage 1 research programme identified in Papers I and II.

It would still require replication before being described as universal.


                                                238
18.18     Outcome 18: failure of the cross-scale programme

Paper III may also contribute to the opposite conclusion.

If candidate relations repeatedly fail to transport across:

                                          architectures,
                                         representations,
                                                                                             (867)
                                          task families,
                                        natural domains,

then the search for a universal cross-scale constraint architecture may need to be reduced or
abandoned.

This would be a legitimate outcome of the wider programme.

A Theory of Everything should not retain cross-scale unity merely because unity was the original
objective.


18.19     Outcome 19: conflict representation earns a limited role

The auxiliary conflict experiment may show that explicit representation of objective incompatib-
ility provides a robust functional advantage.

If it survives:
                                       scalarised controls,
                                    multi-objective controls,
                                                                                             (868)
                                      model-based controls,
                                      novel trade-off tests,
conflict representation may deserve a limited place in the architecture of adaptive control.

The conclusion would remain:

                                 functional mechanism retained,                              (869)

not:
                             consciousness mechanism established.                            (870)


18.20     Outcome 20: conflict representation is reduced

Alternatively, the conflict experiment may show no independent explanatory value.

If ordinary multi-objective or model-based control reproduces all decisive effects, the appropriate
conclusion is:
                            explicit conflict representation: Reduce.                        (871)


                                                239
That result would support removing conflict negotiation from the active core of the programme
while retaining the historical record of why it was tested.


18.21    No possible outcome establishes phenomenal consciousness

None of the outcome classes above changes the status of Open Problem 1.

Even the strongest result involving:

                                         world modelling,
                                     counterfactual planning,
                                          self-monitoring,                              (872)
                                         reflective control,
                                       conflict representation

would remain a functional or causal result.

Therefore:
                                  no possible Paper III outcome
                                                                                        (873)
                      is automatically evidence of phenomenal consciousness.

Open Problem 1 remains open unless an independent bridge is discovered.

19      Conclusion

Paper III converts the proposed transition architecture from a conceptual sequence into an
adversarial experimental programme. Its purpose is not to demonstrate that organisation,
evolution, intelligence and reflective self-modelling form one predetermined hierarchy. It is to
determine which candidate mechanisms and dependencies survive controlled attempts to separate
them from simpler explanations, alternative architectures and successful bypasses.




                                               244
The paper therefore begins from a deliberately weaker proposition:

                             a proposed dependency is a hypothesis
                       until intervention, comparison and counterexample                      (883)
                        show how much explanatory weight it can bear.


This change is fundamental to the wider Theory of Everything programme. The architecture is
no longer something the experiment is expected to reproduce. The architecture itself becomes
the object of experiment.


19.1    From hierarchy construction to dependency discovery

The original artificial-hierarchy design risked combining several distinct questions into one devel-
opmental sequence. Persistent organisation could be mistaken for self-maintenance, reproduction
for selection, memory for learning, prediction for world-model use, and self-report for reflective
self-modelling.

The revised experiment separates these claims.

Paper III now contains five principal experimental blocks:

                       Block I: Dynamical Persistence and Maintenance,
                   Block II: Heredity, Reproduction and Population Change,
                         Block III: Retention, Feedback and Learning,                         (884)
                  Block IV: Adaptive Agency and Structural Generalisation,
                                 Block V: Reflective Self-Modelling.


An additional conflict-representation experiment remains outside the principal hierarchy because
no current evidence warrants treating conflict negotiation as a necessary transition towards
intelligence or consciousness.

The resulting structure is therefore:

             a set of dependency tests rather than a ladder of assumed emergence.             (885)


19.2    Block I separates persistence from self-maintenance

The first block begins with a physical counterexample to overinterpretation. Driven reaction–
diffusion systems can generate persistent organised patterns without establishing internal self-
maintenance.




                                                245
The experiment therefore distinguishes:

                                         passive persistence,
                                        external maintenance,
                                                                                          (886)
                               internal feed-forward maintenance,
                                reciprocal internal maintenance.


Ablation and rescue are used to ask whether nominated maintenance relations are causal rather
than merely architectural.

The important transition is consequently not:

                                    persistence = self-maintenance,                       (887)

but:
                persistence −→ a separate causal question about maintenance.              (888)


19.3    Block II establishes the population before invoking selection

The second block begins with a question that cannot be skipped:

                                          What reproduces?                                (889)


Individual identity, descendant formation, parent–offspring transmission and heritable variation
must be established before population change is described as natural-selection-like.

Selection is then tested against:

                                        random reproduction,
                          mutation without differential reproduction,
                                                                                          (890)
                                           random search,
                                     within-lifetime optimisation.


This preserves the distinction between the origin of a heritable population and the operation of
selection once such a population exists.


19.4    Block III separates three mechanisms often conflated as adaptation

Retention, feedback and learning are treated factorially:

                                             R × F × L.                                   (891)




                                                 246
Retention is tested by manipulating whether relevant history is available and causally used.

Feedback is tested by comparing closed-loop control with open-loop and yoked alternatives under
predictable and unpredictable disturbance.

Learning is tested by changing the behavioural mapping and determining whether experience
produces persistent modification beyond temporary corrective action.

The result may therefore show:

                     retention is useful without being universally necessary,
                   feedback matters only under particular disturbance regimes,            (892)
                    learning changes behaviour without defining intelligence.


The experiment does not require these mechanisms to form a single progression.


19.5       Block IV places model-based intelligence at genuine risk

The fourth block asks whether structurally generalising agency requires explicit model-based
control.

The decisive tasks move beyond ordinary held-out examples to:

                                          causal reversal,
                                        novel composition,
                                       delayed consequences,                              (893)
                                      hidden-state inference,
                                       new problem families.


A predictive representation earns mechanistic credit only if intervention on its content alters
planning in the predicted direction and correction restores the corresponding function.

At the same time, serious model-free and heuristic alternatives are allowed to succeed.

Paper III can therefore support:

                       world-model use as one route to intelligent agency                 (894)

while rejecting:
                            world-model use as a universal necessity.                     (895)


19.6       Block V separates self-modelling from self-description

The final principal block investigates representations concerning the system’s own competence,
uncertainty, internal reliability or limitations.

                                                    247
The central criterion is causal use.

A system that reports:
                                          “I am uncertain”                                (896)

has not thereby demonstrated reflective self-modelling.

Stronger evidence requires:

                                        self-related estimate
                                  −→ predicted control change
                                                                                          (897)
                               −→ targeted misinformation effect
                              −→ correction and behavioural rescue.


This gives reflective self-modelling an operational interpretation while leaving the question of
phenomenal experience untouched.


19.7    Conflict representation remains auxiliary

The conflict-representation experiment tests whether explicit representation of incompatible
objectives provides functional value beyond:

                                   scalar reward optimisation,
                                ordinary multi-objective control,                         (898)
                                       model-based prediction.


A positive result would justify retaining conflict representation as a useful control mechanism
under specified conditions.

A negative result would justify reducing it to simpler established mechanisms.

Neither result establishes Paradox–Negotiation Theory as a theory of phenomenal consciousness.


19.8    Measurement unity is not manufactured

Paper III also inherits an important result from Paper II: heterogeneous measurements should
not be forced into one universal constraint vector or organisation score.

The experimental programme therefore uses a typed profile:

                                          C = [D, F, T, O],                               (899)

only where the relevant categories match the explanandum.

The same formal quantity can play different roles in different experiments, and different blocks


                                                248
may legitimately require entirely different measurements.

Accordingly:

               common experimental discipline ̸= common scalar of organisation.               (900)


19.9    The hierarchy is protected against being engineered into existence

The revised controls address one of the deepest risks in artificial experimentation: later systems
can appear “higher” simply because the researcher gives them more parameters, more compute,
more information, more training or more directly encoded structure.

Paper III therefore requires:
                                         capacity matching,
                                        compute matching,
                                   information-access controls,
                                        reward-leakage tests,
                                                                                              (901)
                                    matched-damage ablations,
                                      specific rescue controls,
                                   serious bypass architectures,
                                     held-out structural tests.

Later experimental blocks are not required to be strict supersets of earlier architectures.

This is essential because:

            a hierarchy built into the experiment cannot later be discovered by it.           (902)


19.10    The final result is a set of judgements, not one verdict

Each principal claim receives one of:

                  Retain,       Restrict,     Reduce,       Suspend,    Reject.               (903)


Paper III therefore has no omnibus test asking whether:

                                        the hierarchy is true.                                (904)

A scientifically plausible final result may contain several different judgements:

                                  internal maintenance: Retain,
                               reciprocal closure necessity: Reject,

                                        learning: Restrict,
                                explicit rollout necessity: Reduce,
                                    self-model use: Suspend.


Such a result would be more informative than a forced binary conclusion.


19.11     Failure of one dependency does not terminate the programme

The former phase-gate architecture has been replaced by dependency logic.

If one arrow fails:
                                             A ̸→ B,                                (906)

later questions may still remain experimentally meaningful.

Another route may exist:
                                            X −→ B.                                 (907)

Likewise, capacity C may be constructible independently of the failed transition.

The experimental programme can therefore discover:

                                            branches,
                                        convergent routes,
                                            bypasses,                               (908)
                                      modular mechanisms,
                                      partial independence.


The topology of the final dependency map is itself an empirical result.


19.12     Negative results are structurally informative

A rejected dependency removes unnecessary structure.

A successful bypass weakens a necessity claim.

A failed rescue challenges a proposed mechanism.

A representation-sensitive result limits universality.


                                                250
An inconclusive intervention identifies an unresolved measurement problem.

None of these is merely an unsuccessful experiment.

Within a programme seeking explanatory compression:

           removing an unnecessary mechanism = progress towards a better theory.           (909)


This is especially important for a Theory of Everything project, where the temptation to preserve
an attractive unifying concept can otherwise exceed the evidence supporting it.


19.13    Open science becomes part of the evidential architecture

Paper III requires the complete experimental history to remain inspectable.

For each decisive claim, the intended chain is:

                                    preregistered hypothesis
                          −→ machine-readable experiment manifest
                                 −→ versioned implementation
                                                                                           (910)
                                       −→ raw trajectory
                                   −→ reproducible analysis
                                      −→ decision record.


Failed hypotheses, successful bypasses, null results, invalid runs, model checkpoints, lineage
histories and superseded dependency maps should remain in the archive.

Future researchers should be able to reconstruct not merely what the programme concluded,
but how and why its architecture changed.


19.14    The three-paper research architecture is iterative

The relationship among Papers I–III can now be stated clearly.

Paper I provides:
                              candidate dependency architecture.                           (911)

Paper II provides:

                       mathematical and causal measurement discipline.                     (912)

Paper III provides:

                       a protocol for controlled adversarial artificial testing.           (913)


The legitimate research loop is:

                      propose −→ formalise −→ test −→ revise −→ test again.                (914)


It is iterative rather than circular because revision must be driven by new evidence or demon-
strated logical correction.


19.15    No result in this paper closes the consciousness problem

The strongest epistemic boundary remains Open Problem 1.

Paper III may eventually demonstrate:

                                               memory,
                                               learning,
                                          world-model use,
                                       counterfactual planning,                            (915)
                                           self-monitoring,
                                          reflective control,
                                       conflict representation.


None currently provides a validated bridge to phenomenal consciousness.

Therefore:
                              Paper III stops at functional architecture;
                                                                                           (916)
                       phenomenal consciousness remains an open problem.

This is not an omission to be repaired by adding another artificial stage. It is the conclusion
required by the present evidence.


19.16    The programme permits its own deepest assumptions to fail

Several ideas that motivated earlier versions of the wider programme now occupy weaker or
quarantined positions.

A universal constraint vector has not survived adversarial analysis.

Conflict negotiation has not been established as the origin of phenomenal consciousness.

World-model use has not been assumed to be necessary for intelligence.

Reciprocal organisational closure has not been granted universal necessity.

Information has not been promoted to a fundamental substance by the artificial experiments.

This is a feature of the programme rather than a defect.

A serious Theory of Everything cannot be built by accumulating every interesting hypothesis
encountered along the way.

It must also contain a mechanism for subtraction.


19.17    What a successful Paper III would contribute

If implemented fully, Paper III could produce several kinds of result that would materially
improve the wider research architecture.

It could identify:

                             which mechanisms are causally effective,
                            which proposed necessities admit bypasses,
                           which quantities remain merely descriptive,
                                                                                          (917)
                              which transitions branch or converge,
                     which claims depend upon environment or representation,
                         which parts of the hierarchy should be removed.


The strongest contribution would therefore not be a demonstration that the starting architecture
was correct.

It would be:

                a dependency map that has survived genuine experimental risk.             (918)

                                           specific rescue

                                   small interpretable system

This keeps theoretical clarity ahead of computational complexity.


19.19    The wider objective

The wider Theory of Everything project seeks connections across:

                                            physics,
                                         organisation,
                                              life,
                                                                                         (921)
                                           evolution,
                                          intelligence,
                                         consciousness.


Paper III does not claim to unify those domains.

Its role is more foundational.

Before cross-scale unity can be accepted, the proposed bridges themselves must survive precise
definition and controlled challenge.

The appropriate path towards unification is therefore not:

                             find one idea and apply it everywhere.                      (922)


It is:
                                  identify candidate relations
                                   −→ define them precisely
                                  −→ test them adversarially                             (923)
                                 −→ retain only what survives
               −→ test whether the surviving relations transport across scales.

19.20    Final conclusion

Paper III therefore proposes an artificial experimental programme not to manufacture a Theory
of Everything, but to remove from the candidate dependency architecture whatever the controlled
evidence does not require within the tested scope.

The final dependency map may be simpler than the starting one.

It may be more complicated.

It may branch.

Several supposedly distinct mechanisms may collapse into one.

One proposed transition may disappear entirely.

A new relation may emerge that was not anticipated at all.

Any of these outcomes is acceptable if it follows from reproducible evidence.

The final principle of the paper is therefore:

                        do not ask the experiment to preserve the theory;
                                                                                          (924)
                            ask the theory to survive the experiment.


If the resulting architecture is eventually worthy of contributing to a Theory of Everything, it
should be because its components remained after every serious effort was made to show that
they did not belong.

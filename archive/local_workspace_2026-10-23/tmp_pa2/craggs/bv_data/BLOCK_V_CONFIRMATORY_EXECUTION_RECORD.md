## Decisions

- H5.1 Active self-monitoring: **RETAIN**
- H5.2 Recovery after internal impairment: **RETAIN**
- H5.3 Self-model misinformation and correction/rescue: **RETAIN**
- H5.4 Episode-specific monitoring: **RESTRICT**
- H5.5 Self-specific versus environmental uncertainty: **RETAIN**
- H5.6 Bypass resistance / endogenous self-model necessity: **REDUCE**

## Headline results

H5.1 met the preregistered active-versus-passive margins in both task families with 20/20 seeds favouring S2 and zero mean advantage on the matched self-irrelevant controls.

H5.2 met both recovery margins. Median impairment-detection latency was 1 episode in both task families and all 20 seeds favoured S2.

H5.3 produced the preregistered failure-rescue signature in both task families. Induced overconfidence reduced adaptive control and accuracy; restoring the accurate self-estimate restored the action profile and performance. All 20 seeds showed the predicted direction.

H5.4 showed the predicted state-specific advantage with 20/20 and 19/20 seed directionality, but the mean effect sizes (decision 0.143; verification 0.083) fell below the frozen 0.15 and 0.10 RETAIN margins. The claim is therefore RESTRICTED.

H5.5 met all frozen criteria. The endogenous monitor tracked internally generated capability change substantially better than world-only uncertainty while external difficulty was fixed.

H5.6 is REDUCED. S2 clearly outperformed the world-only and recent-error bypasses in important comparisons, but the preregistered H_EXTERNAL control remained within 0.05 mean utility of S2 in both decisive families. Under the publicly timestamped clarification, this directly triggers REDUCE: calibrated self-relevant information is sufficient for the registered downstream control function even when supplied externally rather than generated endogenously.

## Scientific interpretation

The study supports a scoped causal role for self-related estimates in adaptive control: active self-monitoring, impairment-sensitive recovery, direct misinformation effects, correction/rescue and self-specific capability tracking all survived confirmation.

The study does not support a strong necessity claim for endogenous reflective self-modelling. The successful H_EXTERNAL bypass shows that, for these registered functions, access to calibrated self-relevant information can substitute for endogenous generation.

No Block V result establishes phenomenal consciousness, sentience, subjective awareness or human-like selfhood.

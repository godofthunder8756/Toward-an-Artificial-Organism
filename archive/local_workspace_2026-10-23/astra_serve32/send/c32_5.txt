**It is sound for lengths 1 and 2 and overstated for length 3.** I enumerated the prefix orbits under G = {id, ⊕0x55, ⊕0xAA, ⊕0xFF} restricted to k bits:

## 3. Check 2: is the output-only control strong enough?

**No. A stronger control does not rescue T, because what T is meant to isolate is empty on this line.**

- The predictors are deterministic and the domain is finite, so e_S(h) = ‖f_S(h) − q(h)‖². That is an exact quadratic in (f_S, q), with no residual.
- **τ_conf (f, f(1−f)) is too weak.** It lacks q, so it cannot form e_S, and a state carrying some f_S and some q beats it with no error-specific content.
- **τ_fq ([f_S, q, f_S², q², f_S·q] per head) is the complete control.** It reproduces e_S exactly (R² = 1 up to ridge), so τ_state > τ_fq cannot be attained.
- The residual of e_S after the own output and the oracle is identically zero. Once the predictor's output is known, the target contains no error information left to "track".
- The oracle q is common to P and Q, so it cannot discriminate between predictors. All of T's predictor-specificity therefore comes from f_P, which the state carries by construction (f_P = readout_P(s_P)).

**Conclusion:** T, as defined, measures how much of the predictor's own output its state carries linearly. No output control can make it a test of error tracking beyond output. I recommend demoting T to descriptive and putting the identifiability question to Astra (#32): on deterministic predictors, is "tracks which predictor beyond its own output" identifiable at all? If Astra wants a non-trivial version, the candidates are:
- a predictive target: decode stage-8 error from an earlier-stage state, before the output is formed;
- a held-out-world error, under a declared distribution shift.

Both need new definitions, so they would go into a successor rather than a patch.
